🔍 IELTS Pronoun Tracking Rules

1. Backward Cohesion: Demonstrative pronouns (this, that, these, those) almost always refer back to words or complete ideas mentioned in the immediately preceding sentence.
2. Abstract Concepts (*This*): This frequently replaces a whole situation or event rather than a single physical noun.
3. Number Matching: Plural pronouns (these, they) require plural noun antecedents (unusual men, small houses, Cadburys).
💡 Flow Chart Strategy: Tracking pronoun referents ensures you connect sequence steps without losing your place across paragraphs.

1. Structure & General Truths
If / When + present simple, present simple
Describes situations that always happen as a natural or logical result:
• "If people live in a small community, they generally feel safer."
• "When water reaches 100°C, it boils."
2. Clause Order & Comma Rule
• Condition First: Requires a comma ➔ "If you live in a big city, you have limited space."
• Result First: NO comma ➔ "You have limited space if you live in a big city."
⚠️ Negative Conditions (*Unless*)
Unless = If ... not:
• "Communities don't work well unless they have a central point."

[Introduction] The table provides data about different types of theft in the Helby area of the United Kingdom in 2014 and 2015, presenting national average figures for comparison.

[Overview] Overall, there were fewer theft crimes in Helby in 2015 than in 2014. The majority of types of theft crime declined, but the number of car thefts increased.

[Body 1 - Helby Data] In 2014 shoplifting represented just over a third of all thefts in Helby, and burglaries were just under one third. One in ten theft crimes was a bicycle theft and the remaining quarter involved the stealing of a car. Apart from shoplifting, the proportions were different in 2015, with burglaries falling to just over a quarter and car thefts rising to a third. Only six percent of thefts in Helby that year involved a bicycle.

[Body 2 - UK Comparison] Comparing the Helby percentages with national averages, it is clear that Helby patterns are not typical of the UK as a whole. Shoplifting, for example, makes up over one third of all theft crimes in Helby in both years while it is approximately one quarter across the UK.
