Changing habits in accessing the news

[Paragraph 1] There are many different ways to get the news nowadays. Some people buy a daily newspaper, others watch television or listen to the radio and still others choose to get their news online. Many, of course, make regular use of more than one source.

[Paragraph 2] The Pew Research Center in the US recently conducted a survey with 3,612 adults nationwide. Researchers wanted to find out how they currently learn about the news. They ended up classifying the people they interviewed into four groups: Traditionalists, Net-newsers, Integrators and the Disengaged.

[Paragraph 3] The researchers called the largest group Traditionalists. As the name suggests, they prefer to access the news in a traditional way, typically by watching television. They do this a great deal – in the morning, in the afternoon and in the evening. They explain that they understand the news better when they see pictures rather than just reading or listening. Their level of education is relatively low, with over 60 percent of this group having no more than a high school education. This group tends to be middle-aged or older. Most people in this group have a computer at home but they almost never use it to find out about the news. Although they are still a large group – 46 percent of the population – their numbers have been going down for ten years now.

[Paragraph 4] Net-newsers, on the other hand, choose to get the majority of their news from the internet. The smallest of the four groups, they are also the youngest, with a typical age of 35. They are better educated than other groups; eight in ten of them have at least attended college. Nearly 60 percent of the group are men and they also tend to be relatively well-off. Although the internet is by far the main source of news for Net-newsers, they do occasionally use other sources too.

[Paragraph 5] The third group are the Integrators. These are people who regularly use both television and the internet as their source of news. They usually watch the news on television in the evenings and log on to the internet from work. Almost half of them also get some of their news from the radio. Integrators are people with a particularly strong interest in the news. They keep up-to-date with national and international politics and they also follow sports news. They currently represent 23 percent of the population. They typically have higher education and a high salary.

[Paragraph 6] The researchers reported that 14 percent of the US population fall into the fourth and final group, which they called the Disengaged. This group has little or no interest in the news. Almost half of the group do not watch the television news even once a day. In general, they have even fewer academic qualifications than the Traditionalists. There are signs that the proportion of young people in the Disengaged category is rising.

[Paragraph 7] The Pew Center's research study highlights interesting trends in the ways people access news today. One of these is the fact that there are increasing extremes of behaviour in the US population: while many access stories constantly across multiple platforms, the number who pay no attention at all is also growing.

Band 8+ Task 1 Model Breakdown

The charts illustrate the principal source of news for young adults (aged 16–24) and for people over 55 across 26 countries. The charts show that the two age groups access news in rather different ways: a majority of younger people get their news online, while the older generation, in contrast, choose television as their main source.

According to the data, nearly two thirds of younger people (64 percent) choose online news, while only a quarter of older adults do so. Conversely, over half of the over-55s (55 percent) rely on television, compared to just a quarter of the youth demographic.

Traditional print and radio media account for only minor proportions in both groups. Newspapers are used by 12 percent of older adults compared with only 6 percent of younger people, while radio remains the least preferred source for both.

9a Speaking Part 3 · Attitude Markers

Expressing Attitude in Part 3 Discussions

Use attitude adverbs to signal confidence, reported facts, or emotional evaluations.

Attitude Markers Matrix

  • 100% Certain / Evident: clearly, naturally, obviously, without a doubt
  • Reported / Heard: apparently, reportedly
  • Emotional Evaluation: sadly, worryingly, fortunately

Examiner: How has the way we get news changed recently?

Candidate: "Well, clearly, it has changed a lot. In the past people got news from print newspapers and TV once a day. But now we have constant updates, so naturally, everyone expects news instantly. Sadly, it's also easier now to get inaccurate information from social media."

Brain health and technology

[Paragraph A] We live in the digital age but some people say that too much use of technology will have a negative effect on our mental abilities. However, research studies have shown that technology does not necessarily limit mental abilities; it can, in fact, have a positive impact on them.

[Paragraph B] Exercise makes more blood flow to the brain, so it is one of the simplest ways to get the brain working better. People can use technology like heart monitors to check that they are exercising at the right level. Another way is using GPS devices, which allows runners to know what distance they have run and plan future runs.

[Paragraph C] Electronic devices now make it possible to listen to music on public transport, at work, or while exercising. This is another way digital devices make people's mental abilities stronger.

[Paragraph D] Changes in diet also improve brain function. Technology provides a quick way to check nutrients on a smartphone app when shoppers are selecting food, so they choose based on accurate information.

[Paragraph E] Having a network of social relationships is a key factor in keeping older people happy. Social media and video calls make staying in touch easy even if they live on different continents. The internet means it is possible for people who share the same interests to make contact.

[Paragraph F] Learning new things changes the structure of brain neurons and develops more connections between them. The internet offers all sorts of amazing opportunities for study.

Band 8+ Task 1 Model Breakdown

The charts illustrate the proportions of adults in the USA who owned five different types of digital equipment in 2010 and 2015. In 2015 a higher percentage of people owned mobile phones, e-readers and tablets than in 2010. However, ownership of laptops and games consoles declined over the period.

The device that the largest number of people owned in both years was the mobile phone, rising from just over 80 percent to 92 percent. The increase in ownership was greatest for tablets: only 3 percent had one in 2010, in comparison with 45 percent in 2015. A similar upward trend occurred in e-readers (rising from 4% to 19%).

In contrast, ownership of both laptops and games consoles fell over the period. Laptop ownership declined slightly from 79 to 73 percent, and the dip in games console usage was similarly modest (42% to 40%).
