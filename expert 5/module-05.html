🎯 Matching Information Method

1. Identify Information Type: Distinguish between a description, a reason, a comparison, and a personal reaction.
2. Scan for Synonymous Ideas: Do not just match identical words; scan for whole sentence meaning.
3. Note Paragraph Flexibility: Not all paragraphs will contain answers, and some may be used more than once.
1. -ing Forms (Gerunds)
  • As Noun / Subject: Travelling alone can be very hard.
  • After Prepositions: good at learning / feel sad after returning.
  • After Certain Verbs: enjoy, like, hate, can't stand, imagine, avoid.
2. Infinitives (with & without to)
• Infinitive with 'to': After verbs (decide, want, refuse, afford, hope), adjectives (difficult to sleep), or to express purpose (went to Egypt to see...).
• Bare Infinitive (without 'to'): After modal verbs (might visit) and verbs of letting/making (made me go, let us walk).
⚠️ Common Verb Complements
• ❌ He made me to go. ➔ ✅ He made me go.
• ❌ I went for buying food. ➔ ✅ I went to buy food.
• ❌ She is good to speak English. ➔ ✅ She is good at speaking.
1. (walk) around a town is the best way to find interesting places.
2. I can't stand (fly) because there's so little space.
3. (travel) alone can be very hard.
4. I'm good at (learn) new languages when I travel.
5. Some countries refuse you in without a visa.
6. It can be difficult where to go on holiday.
7. They let us round the site for a couple of hours.

💡 Grammar Patterns

  • Sentence Subject: Gerund (-ing)
  • After Adjectives: Infinitive with to
  • After let / make: Bare infinitive

Task 1 Prompt: The graph shows the income from tourism in different regions over the period 1960–2010. Summarise by selecting and reporting main features, and make comparisons.

📋 4-Part Summary Ordering (Ex 2c)

Part 1 (Intro): The graph shows how much money different regions received from tourism between 1960 and 2010.
Part 2 (Overview): Although the graph shows some fluctuations, tourist income generally increased in all areas.
Part 3 (Differences): Europe earned the most ($50B–$200B more), while Africa earned the least ($10B–$20B).
Part 4 (Similarities): Americas, Asia/Pacific and Europe all show stable patterns after 1980 peaks, with sharp rises after 2003.

🌟 Band 7+ Structural Rules

  • Overview: State the macro upward trend across all regions.
  • Grouping: Group by clear contrast (Europe & Asia dominance vs Africa flatline).
Key Verbs: rise, grow, peak, reach a peak, fall, decline, decrease, remain stable, stay constant, level off.
1. Tourist income in Europe reached in 2008.
2. Tourist income in Europe in 1990.
3. Tourist income in Africa over 1997–2002.
4. Tourist income in the Americas during the 1990s.

📊 Trend Classifications

  • Upward: grow, increase, rise, reach a peak
  • Downward: fall, decline, decrease
  • Plateau: remain stable, stay constant, level off

Organisation Strategy (Ex 6a)

Grouped by concern levels: Body 1 covers high concern (water & air), while Body 2 covers lower concern (climate & car emissions).

The graph shows how serious people in China, India, Germany and the USA thought four environmental issues were between 2000 and 2010.

Overall, the graph shows that attitudes towards water shortages, air pollution, climate change and automobile emissions all fluctuated considerably over the period. However, people were less worried about each of the issues in 2010 than in the previous years.

In general, water shortages caused more anxiety in any one year than any other issue, with 70 percent of the population rating it as 'very serious' in 2009. In each year on the graph, either water shortages or air pollution was the problem which the highest proportion of people were very worried about. Concern about air pollution, for example, grew until 2006 before a sharp fall in 2007.

In 2000 climate change was the issue fewest people (around 50 percent) expressed serious concern about. Between 2000 and 2003, there was a slow decline in anxiety about this. Although people were less worried about climate change than car emissions at the beginning of the period, the situation changed in 2006.

🌟 Model Strengths

  • Superlative Precision: "fewest people", "highest proportion".
  • Trend Conjunctions: "grew until 2006 before a sharp fall".
  • Accurate Prepositions: "rating it as", "worried about".
