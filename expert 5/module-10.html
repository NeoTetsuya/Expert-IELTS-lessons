Strategic Rules for Y / N / NG

• Focus on Opinions: Questions test the claims and opinions of the writer, not merely objective facts.
• Strict Order: Statements appear in the same chronological order as the text.
• YES: Statement directly agrees with the writer's explicit view.
• NO: Statement directly contradicts what the writer says.
• NOT GIVEN: Impossible to confirm the writer's opinion from the text.

Worked Example (p. 152 Ex 3b)

"It is surprising that people today rely on new digital devices as much as they do."
→ Answer: NO
Passage evidence: Paragraph 1 says "Naturally enough... people have come to depend on it." The phrase naturally enough demonstrates that the author does not find it surprising!

Relative Pronoun Functions

• who / that (people): "The author who wrote this study specializes in communication."
• which / that (things): "We use gadgets which speed up message transmission."
• where (places): "I prefer to work in places where I can get internet access."
• whose (possession): "Students whose phones ring in class will be asked to leave."
• Omission Rule: If the relative pronoun is the object of the verb, it can be omitted: "The emails (which) I received."
• No Redundant Pronouns: Never repeat the pronoun: "The email which I sent it yesterday."

Task Prompt (p. 158)

"Face-to-face communication is better than other ways of communicating, such as email, phone calls or social networking. To what extent do you agree or disagree? Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples."

Essay Architecture (Option B)
• Intro: Paraphrase prompt on electronic options vs direct speech.
• Body 1: Advantages of modern tools (texting frequency, Skype abroad).
• Body 2: Unique power of face-to-face (body language, tone, affection).
• Conclusion: Balanced synthesis affirming face-to-face as the ultimate contact.
Textbook Model Essay (p. 158)

Nowadays it is no longer necessary to meet face to face in order to have a conversation. We can email, telephone, text message or share our news on a social network. However, many people argue that none of these modern means of communication is as good as talking to someone directly.

In my opinion, modern ways of communication have many advantages. Firstly, the ability to text friends and family probably means that we communicate with them more often than in the past and this has to be a positive thing. Technology like Skype means we can have close and satisfying contact with relatives in other countries. Secondly, using social media means we can make connections with new people in other cultures and this must be good for improving international understanding.

However, it is true that face-to-face communication has many advantages too. There is nothing better than spending a long evening with a friend chatting and laughing, as well as talking about more serious things. You can tell a lot about how someone is feeling through their body language and their tone of voice, and you don't get these from texts or social network posts. Moreover, you can't hug your mum or share a cup of tea with a friend when you're on the phone or the laptop.

In conclusion, I think that the different ways of communication we enjoy today are wonderful and have enriched people's lives a great deal. However, talking to someone face to face remains the best form of contact that we can have with another person.

Functional Categories in Professional Contexts

• Obligation & Necessity: must, have to, need to
"International executives must respect local cultural protocols."
• Advice & Recommendation: should, ought to
"Presenters should speak at a measured pace in multilingual meetings."
• Possibility & Deduction: might, may, could
"A prolonged silence might signify contemplation rather than disagreement."

Task Prompt (p. 164)

"It is almost impossible to keep our traditions in a global community. To what extent do you agree or disagree? Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples."

Essay Architecture (p. 164 Ex 3)
• Para A: Brief explanation of globalization theme & migration.
• Para B: Strong disagreement: Family traditions become more cherished abroad.
• Para C: Intercultural appreciation: Host cities celebrate global festivals.
• Para D: Conclusion: Global contact reinforces rather than weakens heritage.
Textbook Model Essay (p. 164)

It is undoubtedly true that nowadays more and more people are travelling round the world. They do this not just for holidays, but also to live and work abroad. Some people are afraid that this means we will all lose our unique cultures and traditions.

I strongly disagree with this point of view. First of all, we need to think about where we learn these traditions. We usually learn them in our family, so we get in the habit of doing what our family does every year. If we go to live in a different place, there is no doubt that it will feel strange at first. As a result, family traditions can feel even more important to us than they were when we were living in our home country.

Another point is that people in the countries which you go to are often very interested in other countries' traditions. If we look at the example of some big cities, we can see they celebrate everyone's special days, not just those of their own country. London, for example, has big Chinese and West Indian festivals as well as English ones. In other words, people are clearly happy to take on new traditions rather than losing or forgetting their old ones.

To conclude, it is certainly true that many people these days are moving away from their original communities and there is much more contact between people of different cultures than ever before. However, I believe that this may actually be making many traditions stronger rather than weaker.

📖 Reading Competencies
  • Navigate Yes / No / Not Given statements by identifying explicit agreement, direct contradiction, and unmentioned assumptions.
  • Scan for factual details in Short-Answer tasks within strict word limits (NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS).
  • Analyze high-context vs low-context cultural communication distinctions in business texts.
📚 Grammar & Vocab Competencies
  • Use defining relative pronouns (who, which, that, where, whose) with accurate antecedent agreement.
  • Identify when relative pronouns function as grammatical objects and can be omitted without redundancy.
  • Deploy modal auxiliaries (must, should, might, could) with appropriate modality strength.
  • Master intercultural vocabulary (values, beliefs, customs, way of life) and prefix antonyms.
✍️ Writing Task 2 Competencies
  • Construct balanced 4-paragraph opinion and discussion essays.
  • Craft authoritative concluding paragraphs using approved signposts (In conclusion, To sum up).
  • Incorporate adverbs and phrases of certainty (undoubtedly, there is no doubt that, clearly, certainly).
