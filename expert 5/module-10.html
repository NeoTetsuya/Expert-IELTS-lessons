Technology and Communication

[Paragraph 1] Advertisements for the latest smartphones and gadgets say they make communication faster. Naturally, people have come to depend on them, but this has some unfortunate consequences.

[Paragraph 2] Geoffrey Tumlin is the author of a book on communications in our contemporary world. His basic argument – that people today give too much importance to gadgets rather than each other – will surprise many people but it is strong and clear. He says the result of such dependency is that people feel dissatisfied with their experience of using these gadgets to communicate.

[Paragraph 3] Electronic devices make sending messages simpler, but there is more to communication than hitting 'Send'. Real communication does not happen until the other person understands the message, and people forget about this important stage in far too many cases. Tumlin says that adding an extra step – considering whether or not the message is understood – can make a person a much more effective communicator. His argument is that most people are focusing only on efficiency when they approach exchanges. They are in the habit of getting face-to-face conversations over as quickly as possible so they can cross one more job off their to-do list.

[Paragraph 4] Meaningful communication is possible only when the speaker or writer thinks about their goals. 'In contrast with average communicators, great communicators think carefully about what they would like to achieve rather than about what they would like to say,' Tumlin says. By making it easy to share feelings, technology encourages quick posts, but emotional messages often stop people from achieving their communication goals.

[Paragraph 5] One of the greatest problems is that sending a message to lots of people is as easy as sending to one. Tumlin argues that communication gets much more difficult as the number of people receiving a message is increased, because there are more points of view to take into account. Before sending a message to a group, one should ask: 'What's my uncle going to think about this post?' or 'How is this email going to come across to other departments?'

10a Writing Task 2 · Opinion Essay

Face-to-Face vs. Modern Digital Communication

Introduction & Context

Nowadays it is no longer necessary to meet face to face in order to have a conversation. We can email, telephone, text message or share our news on a social network. However, many people argue that none of these modern means of communication is as good as talking to someone directly.

Body 1 (Benefits of Modern Digital Tools)

In my opinion, modern ways of communication have many advantages. Firstly, the ability to text friends and family probably means that we communicate with them more often than in the past. Secondly, using social media means we can make connections with new people in other cultures, which improves international understanding.

Body 2 (Irreplaceable Value of Direct Contact)

However, face-to-face communication has irreplaceable advantages too. You can tell a lot about how someone is feeling through their body language and their tone of voice, which you do not get from texts. Moreover, you cannot hug a family member or share a cup of tea with a friend when you are on the phone or laptop.

Conclusion

In conclusion, although digital communication has enriched our lives a great deal, talking to someone face to face remains the most meaningful form of contact we can experience.

Cultural Diversity in Business Today

[Paragraph A] Many businesses today employ people from a range of different cultures. This means communication issues may cause problems. Business success depends on good communications between a company and its staff.

[Paragraph D] There are less obvious cultural differences that affect communication. There is the issue of personal space, for example: an American is used to being 1.5 metres away, while Germans and the Japanese find that distance uncomfortably close. In European and North American cultures, eye contact shows honesty, while in some Asian cultures too much eye contact can suggest a lack of respect. Greeting people also varies: some shake hands or kiss, while others simply say hello.

[Paragraph E] Sociologists divide cultures into 'high-context' and 'low-context'. High-context cultures (Japan, India, Middle East) give great importance to nonverbal communication and body language. Low-context cultures (US, UK, Germany) emphasize the importance of language for communication. Neither type, of course, is better than the other; the important thing is simply to be aware of the difference in values.

[Paragraph F] Cultural differences relating to roles and status also have an impact: different attitudes to gender roles can affect how a male employee feels about having a female manager.

[Paragraph H] If a business values the cultural diversity of its staff and encourages respect, it will be more productive and a happier place for everyone.

10b Writing Task 2 · Opinion Essay

Globalisation & Preserving Cultural Traditions

Introduction & Thesis

It is undoubtedly true that nowadays more people are travelling round the world to live and work abroad. Some people are afraid that this means we will lose our unique cultures and traditions. However, I believe that international contact may actually make many local traditions stronger rather than weaker.

Body 1 (Threats of Uniformity)

On the one hand, global business and media have made different countries more similar. Global chains and entertainment products are available in almost every city. Consequently, younger generations are adopting global consumer habits, raising valid concerns that traditional ways of life may disappear.

Body 2 (Heritage Renewal & Pride)

However, on the other hand, the global community has a positive impact on preserving heritage. For instance, the internet allows communities to document and share their unique cultural customs globally, sparking renewed pride in ancestral languages and historical traditions.

Conclusion

To conclude, although international mobility is greater than ever before, intercultural contact often highlights the irreplaceable value of cultural diversity.
