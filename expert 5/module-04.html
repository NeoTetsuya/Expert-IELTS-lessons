"Monitoring Fitness" & Matching Sentence Endings

Monitoring Fitness

[Paragraph A] Technology has become an important aspect of life for people who want to get and stay fit. They use apps on their smartphone or other devices, for example, to count the number of steps they take every day, to measure their heart rate, to record their sleep patterns and to work out how many calories they consume.

[Paragraph B] Sports scientists recognise that deciding to use a fitness tracker or app is good because it means a person is curious about health and keen to improve their habits. If the attractive design of a device or app encourages an interest in keeping fit, its impact is clearly positive. After all, many people have reported that fitness devices have motivated them to get off the sofa and go for a walk after dinner.

[Paragraph C] Unfortunately, however, many of those after-dinner walkers have added that when their devices broke and they no longer knew how many steps they had taken, there seemed little point in heading out for that walk. Consequently, sports scientists agree that people need to be aware of the fact that apps alone are not enough.

[Paragraph D] One of the problems with fitness trackers is that they usually measure very limited pieces of information – steps taken, movements when asleep or heart rate, for example – and this does not give a clear overall picture of a person’s health. They may record that someone worked out for two hours on a rowing machine, but not that they then spent the rest of the day eating pizza and watching DVDs. In other words, fitness apps can help people to deceive themselves about how healthy their lifestyle is.

[Paragraph E] What sports psychologists recommend users of electronic fitness devices to do is raise their awareness of how their body looks. No one actually needs an app to tell them that they have had a bad night’s sleep; the bags under their eyes can do that just as well. And if people want to lose weight, they may find it more effective to take a regular look at themselves in the mirror rather than try to motivate themselves to diet by checking everything they eat against a calorie counting app. Unfortunately, tracking the calories is also ineffective because the body metabolises calories from sugar differently than those from fat.

[Paragraph F] Keeping a fitness diary is also useful. In it, people should record what they ate and what their mood was two or three hours later. Tracking calories in a food app after eating a large tub of ice cream is not going to solve anyone’s problems. Reflecting on how bad they felt after eating it will. Keeping a fitness diary helps people to eat well and exercise not for the sake of meeting the app’s artificial targets, but because exercise makes everyone feel, look and be better.

4a Grammar Masterclass 05 / 18

Past Simple vs. Past Continuous

Mastering completed actions, progressive background scenes, and interrupted actions.

1. Past Simple (Completed Actions)
Used for actions, states, or habits that started and finished in the past.
  • Specific Time Markers: yesterday, last week, three years ago, in 2018.
  • Consecutive Actions: Actions happening one after another ("I fell over and hurt my leg.").
  • Form: Subject + Verb-ed (Regular) / Irregular form. Negative: didn't + base verb.
• I lost weight last year.
• People didn't have modern medicines 150 years ago.
2. Past Continuous (Actions in Progress)
Used for actions happening at a specific time, parallel actions, or background scenes.
  • Specific Point in Time: "At 8 o'clock this morning I was working out."
  • Parallel Actions (with while): "I was studying while they were relaxing."
  • Form: Subject + was/were + Verb-ing. Negative: wasn't/weren't + Verb-ing.
• The sun was just rising as we started running.
• They weren't following a healthy diet.
⚡ Interrupted Actions & Stative Verb Rules:
• Interruption: Longer ongoing action (Past Continuous) interrupted by a short event (Past Simple):
"I was waiting for the bus when it began to rain."
"While I was swimming, somebody stole my bag."
• Stative Verbs: Never use stative verbs (know, understand, believe, want, like) in continuous forms:
✓ "I didn't understand what he said." (NOT *wasn't understanding*)

4a Writing Task 2 11 / 18

Paraphrasing in Introductions & Model Gym Essay

Master the 3-sentence introduction structure for "Do advantages outweigh disadvantages?" prompts.

The 3-Sentence Introduction Formula

Sentence 1: Paraphrased Context
Introduce the general topic area using synonyms and grammatical rephrasing (e.g. "taking out a gym membership" for "joining a gym").
Sentence 2: Stating the Debate
Acknowledge that there are conflicting perspectives (e.g. "belonging to a gym has both positive and negative aspects...").
Sentence 3: Clear Thesis / Direction
Outline the analytical direction (e.g. "It is sensible to evaluate both aspects before paying membership fees.").

Model Introduction Breakdown

Prompt: Some people keep fit by joining a gym but gyms can be crowded and costly. Do advantages outweigh disadvantages?
Sentence 1: Paraphrased Context

"Many people nowadays decide that taking out a gym membership will be an ideal way for them to stay healthy."

Sentence 2: Acknowledging Both Sides

"However, belonging to a gym has both positive and negative aspects and although some people are happy with this decision, others quickly regret it."

Sentence 3: Thesis / Plan

"It is, therefore, sensible to consider what these aspects are before paying membership fees."

"Remedies from Nature" & Sentence Matching

Read the text about biodiversity in modern pharmacology and complete statements 1–6.

Remedies from Nature

[Paragraph A] Animal and plant species have given people important medicines such as quinine and aspirin, as well as many cancer and HIV-fighting drugs. People have used plants and animals as sources of medicine for thousands of years. For example, medicinal plants that people used over 60,000 years ago were found in an Iraqi cave site. A fur strap found on the arm of a 5,000 year-old Ice Man from the Alps contained a type of fungus which is able to kill bacteria.

[Paragraph B] Most people are amazed to discover that our dependence on nature for health has not reduced. Over the past quarter century, more than half of all the products that drug companies have developed actually use, or copy, substances from the natural world. Moreover, the WHO estimates that in many developing countries, 80 percent of the population relies on traditional medicines from natural sources.

[Paragraph C] However, scientists generally believe that researchers have fully examined less than one percent of all species in order to discover their possible uses in medical treatments. They believe that nature still holds many valuable cures for research to discover. In particular, they point to the importance of tropical rainforests.

[Paragraph D] Unfortunately, the ecosystems that provide some of the world’s most important drugs, such as rainforests and coral reefs, are also the ecosystems that are most at risk today. Warming temperatures mean few coral reefs will remain. Meanwhile, rainforests vanish at an alarming rate – losing more than 320 km² daily. The inevitable result is that many species with important medicinal powers will become extinct.

[Paragraph E] Another problem is that most of nature’s medicines do not come from big and beautiful mammals, such as tigers and elephants. Instead, they come from the least popular of the world’s ecosystems: plants, fungi and invertebrates. Some particularly valuable species are often either poisonous or so small that we cannot see them without a microscope.

[Paragraph F] An additional problem is the fact that medical schools teach their students very little about the discovery of new treatments. Few young doctors, for example, know that captopril comes from the poison of a Brazilian snake. Antibiotics are a miracle drug but doctors use far too many because they do not realise how closely connected they are with nature.

4b Paragraph Coherence 15 / 18

Paragraph Coherence: Topic Sentences & Logic Flow

Order scrambled sentences into a unified academic body paragraph (Topic ➔ Detail ➔ Explanation ➔ Result).

Scrambled Sentences

[Sentence A]: Poor people may decide to take part in a drugs experiment because pharmaceutical companies usually pay participants.
[Sentence B]: As a result, they may even become seriously ill themselves.
[Sentence C]: This may encourage them to agree to do things that they know are not good for their bodies.
[Sentence D]: Testing new medicines on people can have serious consequences.

Analytical Ordering

Position 1 (Topic Sentence):
D introduces the main topic: Danger of experimenting on humans.
Position 2 (Supporting Detail):
A explains why: Financial payments attract vulnerable individuals.
Position 3 (Explanation):
C links payment: Encourages agreeing to risky procedures.
Position 4 (Final Result):
B details result: "As a result, they may even become seriously ill..."
4b Task 2 Model Essay

Full Model Essay: Animal Testing of Medicines

16 / 17
Task Prompt

"Some believe it is wrong to test new medicines on animals. Do the advantages of this practice outweigh the disadvantages?"

Detailed Essay Plan
• Intro: Paraphrase prompt + state that both sides will be considered.
• Body 1 (Disadvantages): Ethical cruelty & biological differences.
• Body 2 (Advantages): Medical breakthroughs & safety validation before humans.
• Conclusion: Advantages of human treatment safety outweigh disadvantages.
Band 6+ Official Model Answer

Many people think that using animals is the best way to find out if new medicines work. Others, however, disagree, saying that it is not right to make use of animals in this way. Let us consider both sides of the argument.

It is certainly true that there are disadvantages to using animals to test new medicines. Firstly, it is cruel. Is an animal’s life worth less than that of a human? Animals are living creatures too and we should respect them. Secondly, a new medicine may not work in the same way on an animal as it does on a person. So the conclusions the researchers come to may be wrong.

However, it is very important to test new medicines. Scientists need to develop new medicines so that doctors can improve the quality of life for millions of people all over the world. Giving new medicines to animals will show that they work well. These experiments are also necessary to make sure that the new medicines pharmaceutical companies develop do not have any serious side effects. It is too risky to experiment with new drugs on humans but if chemists are not able to test them, then sick children and adults will not be able to benefit from new treatments.

I would therefore argue that although it is not good if animals suffer, the advantages of using them to test new medicines outweigh the disadvantages. It is very important for the future of medical science.
