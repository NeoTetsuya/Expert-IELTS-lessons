#### Reading Passage: "Monitoring Fitness"
*Do fitness apps really work? Are they helpful? Or do they drown out the conversation people should be having with their bodies?*

##### Paragraph A
Technology has become an important aspect of life for people who want to get and stay fit. They use apps on their smartphone or other devices, for example, to count the number of steps they take every day, to measure their heart rate, to record their sleep patterns and to work out how many calories they consume.

##### Paragraph B
Sports scientists recognise that deciding to use a fitness tracker or app is good because it means a person is curious about health and keen to improve their habits. If the attractive design of a device or app encourages an interest in keeping fit, its impact is clearly positive. After all, many people have reported that fitness devices have motivated them to get off the sofa and go for a walk after dinner.

##### Paragraph C
Unfortunately, however, many of those after-dinner walkers have added that when their devices broke and they no longer knew how many steps they had taken, there seemed little point in heading out for that walk. Consequently, sports scientists agree that people need to be aware of the fact that apps alone are not enough.

##### Paragraph D
One of the problems with fitness trackers is that they usually measure very limited pieces of information – steps taken, movements when asleep or heart rate, for example – and this does not give a clear overall picture of a person’s health. They may record that someone worked out for two hours on a rowing machine, but not that they then spent the rest of the day eating pizza and watching DVDs. In other words, fitness apps can help people to deceive themselves about how healthy their lifestyle is.

##### Paragraph E
What sports psychologists recommend users of electronic fitness devices to do is raise their awareness of how their body looks. No one actually needs an app to tell them that they have had a bad night’s sleep; the bags under their eyes can do that just as well. And if people want to lose weight, they may find it more effective to take a regular look at themselves in the mirror rather than try to motivate themselves to diet by checking everything they eat against a calorie counting app. Unfortunately, tracking the calories is also ineffective because the body metabolises calories from sugar differently than those from fat.

##### Paragraph F
Keeping a fitness diary is also useful. In it, people should record what they ate and what their mood was two or three hours later. Tracking calories in a food app after eating a large tub of ice cream is not going to solve anyone’s problems. Reflecting on how bad they felt after eating it will. Keeping a fitness diary helps people to eat well and exercise not for the sake of meeting the app’s artificial targets, but because exercise makes everyone feel, look and be better.
*(Note: metabolise means to change food in the body into energy and new cells.)*

### 2. Language Development: Past Simple & Past Continuous

The past simple and past continuous are used to discuss actions, events, and situations in the past.

#### Past Simple vs. Past Continuous Reference Matrix

| Aspect | Past Simple | Past Continuous |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Core Function** | Actions, states, or completed events that started and finished in the past at a specific time. | Actions in progress at a specific past moment, background scenes, or two continuous actions running simultaneously. |
| **Form (+)** | Subject + `verb-ed` (or irregular past form)<br>*I worked out yesterday.* | Subject + `was/were` + `verb-ing`<br>*I was working out at 8 a.m.* |
| **Form (-)** | Subject + `didn't` + base verb<br>*I didn't lose much weight.* | Subject + `wasn't/weren't` + `verb-ing`<br>*They weren't following a healthy diet.* |
| **Form (?)** | `Did` + Subject + base verb?<br>*Did you take your medicine?* | `Was/Were` + Subject + `verb-ing`?<br>*Were you sleeping when I called?* |
| **Key Time Words** | *yesterday, last week, three years ago, in 1995, during the winter, when* | *at 8 o'clock, while, as, all morning, throughout the year* |

#### Special Interaction Rules
1. **Interrupted Actions**: A continuous background action (Past Continuous) is interrupted by a shorter, sudden action (Past Simple).
   * *Example*: *"I was jogging in the park when I twisted my ankle."*
   * *Example*: *"While they were training for the marathon, it began to rain."*
   * *Rule*: We generally use **while** before the Past Continuous clause and **when** before the Past Simple clause.
2. **Consecutive Actions**: When two or more events occur sequentially one after another, both verbs take the **Past Simple**.
   * *Example*: *"She arrived at the clinic, registered at reception, and saw the doctor."*
3. **Stative Verbs**: Verbs expressing states, feelings, or mental processes (*know, understand, believe, want, like, hate, need, belong*) are **never** used in continuous forms.
   * *Correct*: *"I knew the answer."* (NOT: *"I was knowing the answer."*)

### 3. Vocabulary: Illnesses, Symptoms, Injuries & Treatments

Precise terminology regarding medical situations and stay-healthy habits is heavily tested across IELTS Listening, Reading, Speaking, and Writing.

#### Illnesses and Symptoms
Match the terms from the word box to their medical descriptions:
* **allergy**: A physiological reaction to food, pollen, or surroundings causing itchy skin, rashes, or runny noses.
* **heart attack**: A severe cardiovascular emergency marked by acute chest pain and difficulty breathing.
* **stress**: Feeling constantly tired, anxious, or worried due to work or lifestyle pressures.
* **food poisoning**: A gastrointestinal illness caused by consuming bacteria-contaminated food.
* **asthma**: A chronic respiratory disease causing coughing and severe breathing difficulties.
* **infection**: An invasion of body tissues by bacteria or viruses (such as throat, ear, or chest infections).
* **flu**: A viral respiratory illness marked by high body temperatures, body aches, chills, and fatigue.
* **cold**: A mild viral infection causing sneezing, minor coughing, and runny noses.

#### Accidents & Injuries
* **injury**: Physical damage to a part of the body resulting from an accident (e.g., *suffer a serious spinal injury*).
* **burns**: Tissue damage caused by exposure to heat, open flame, boiling liquids, or steam.
* **bruise**: A dark purple or blue mark on the skin caused by a physical blow breaking underlying capillaries.
* **bites**: Puncture wounds from animal or insect jaws (e.g., *mosquito bites, dog bites*).
* **stings**: Puncture wounds with venom from insect stingers (e.g., *bee stings, wasp stings*).
* **break / fracture**: Fracturing a bone (e.g., *break a leg, fracture a wrist*).
* **cut**: Severing the skin with a sharp edge or blade (e.g., *cut oneself with a kitchen knife*).

#### Getting Better & Medical Treatments

2. **surgery**: An invasive medical procedure in which surgeons cut open the body to repair, remove, or adjust organs/tissues.

### 4. Writing: Opinion Essays & Paraphrasing in Introductions

In IELTS Writing Task 2, candidates must demonstrate grammatical range and lexical precision by paraphrasing the words in the essay prompt for their introduction.

#### Paraphrasing Principles
An effective introduction consists of 3 distinct functional elements:
1. **Background statement**: Rephrases the general topic using high-level synonyms and different grammatical structures.
2. **Perspective contrast**: Highlights the debate (e.g., both sides of an issue or differing public opinions).
3. **Thesis statement**: Outlines the writer's stance or indicates the scope of the essay.

##### Exercise 3: Paraphrase Evaluation
Evaluate the strong and weak paraphrases below:

1. **Prompt**: *Some people believe that all children should have to do some kind of organised physical activity every day.*
   * **Strong Paraphrase (A)**: *Many people argue that some form of physical exercise should be a daily part of every child’s life.* (Accurately preserves meaning with rich synonyms).
   * **Weak Paraphrase (B)**: *Some people believe that teachers need to give all children regular lessons in how to play sports well.* (Incorrectly limits the scope to school sports lessons).
2. **Prompt**: *Many universities encourage students to walk and cycle by not allowing them to bring cars to the campus.*
   * **Strong Paraphrase (B)**: *A number of higher education institutions persuade undergraduates to walk or cycle by banning cars on university grounds.* (Excellent vocabulary synonyms and syntactic shift).
   * **Weak Paraphrase (A)**: *The majority of students prefer not to drive to university because parking is very difficult.* (Distorts the meaning from an institutional policy to student preferences).

##### Exercise 4: Phrase Synonym Mapping
* *"aged from five to eighteen"* $\rightarrow$ **schoolchildren of all ages**
* *"make them fitter and healthier"* $\rightarrow$ **help improve their fitness**
* *"daily"* $\rightarrow$ **every day**
* *"pupils"* $\rightarrow$ **schoolchildren**
* *"physical education"* $\rightarrow$ **a sports lesson**

#### Model Introduction: The Gym Essay
*Prompt: Some people choose to keep fit by joining a gym but gyms can be crowded and expensive. Do you think the advantages of joining a gym outweigh the disadvantages?*

> "Many people nowadays decide that taking out a gym membership will be an ideal way for them to stay healthy. However, belonging to a gym has both positive and negative aspects and although some people are happy with this decision, others quickly regret it. It is, therefore, sensible to consider what these aspects are before paying membership fees."

##### Structural Analysis of a High-Scoring Introduction:
1. **Sentence 1**: *"Many people nowadays decide..."* $\rightarrow$ Introduces the general topic area in the writer's own words.
2. **Sentence 2**: *"However, belonging to a gym has..."* $\rightarrow$ Suggests how opinions on the topic differ, introducing both advantages and disadvantages.
3. **Sentence 3**: *"It is, therefore, sensible..."* $\rightarrow$ Thesis statement establishing the analytical stance that will guide the body paragraphs.

#### Reading Passage: "Remedies from Nature"

##### Paragraph A
Animal and plant species have given people important medicines such as quinine and aspirin, as well as many cancer and HIV-fighting drugs. People have used plants and animals as sources of medicine for thousands of years. For example, medicinal plants that people used over 60,000 years ago were found in an Iraqi cave site. A fur strap found on the arm of a 5,000 year-old Ice Man from the Alps contained a type of *fungus which is able to kill bacteria.

##### Paragraph B
Most people are amazed to discover that our dependence on nature for health has not reduced. Over the past quarter century, more than half of all the products that drug companies have developed actually use, or copy, substances from the natural world. Moreover, the World Health Organization estimates that in many developing countries, 80 percent of the population relies on traditional medicines from natural sources.

##### Paragraph C
However, scientists generally believe that researchers have fully examined less than one percent of all species in order to discover their possible uses in medical treatments. They believe that nature still holds many valuable cures for research to discover. In particular, they point to the importance of tropical rainforests as a potential source of new medicines. Although rainforests cover only six percent of the earth’s land surface, they contain over half of its biodiversity.

##### Paragraph D
Unfortunately, the ecosystems that provide some of the world’s most important drugs, such as rainforests and coral reefs, are also the ecosystems that are most at risk today. There are concerns that warming temperatures mean that few coral reefs will remain by the end of the century. Meanwhile, agriculture and various major development projects are making the world’s rainforests vanish at an alarming rate – the world is losing more than 320 km² every day. The inevitable result is that many species with important medicinal powers will become extinct.

##### Paragraph E
Another problem is that most of nature’s medicines do not come from big and beautiful mammals, such as tigers and elephants. Instead, they come from the least popular of the world’s ecosystems: plants, fungi and *invertebrates. Some particularly valuable species are often either poisonous or so small that we cannot see them without a microscope. This makes campaigning for their *preservation much more difficult.

##### Paragraph F
An additional problem is the fact that medical schools teach their students very little about the discovery of new treatments. Few young doctors, for example, know that the blood pressure medicine captopril, one of the best-selling drugs of all time, comes from the poison of a Brazilian snake. Antibiotics, most of which come from nature, are a miracle drug but doctors use far too many of them. This is partly because they do not realise the dangers involved or how closely connected these drugs are with the natural world.

##### Paragraph G
Nature and medicine are closely connected. We must protect nature. If we don’t, we not only risk losing many plant and animal species but we are also risking our own health. In all the discussions about saving the world’s biodiversity from extinction, people often forget one point: the world’s species provide people with a large number of life-saving medicines.

##### Explanatory Notes:
* ***fungus**: A simple type of plant with no leaves or flowers that grows on surfaces.*
* ***biodiversity**: The total variety of plants and animals in a particular habitat.*
* ***invertebrate**: A species of animal that does not possess a backbone.*
* ***preservation**: The act of keeping something in its original state or safe from harm.*

### 3. Writing: Animal Testing Task 2 Essay (Organising Ideas & Topic Sentences)

#### Paragraph Coherence & Topic Sentences
A well-structured IELTS body paragraph must open with a **topic sentence** that outlines the main point of the paragraph. All subsequent sentences must support, explain, or illustrate that point.

##### Paragraph Ordering Exercise
Reorder the scrambled sentences (A–D) to form a coherent, logical body paragraph:
* **Sentence A**: *Poor people may decide to take part in a drugs experiment because pharmaceutical companies usually pay participants.*
* **Sentence B**: *As a result, they may even become seriously ill themselves.*
* **Sentence C**: *This may encourage them to agree to do things that they know are not good for their bodies.*
* **Sentence D**: *Testing new medicines on people can have serious consequences.*

##### Correct Order:
1. **Sentence D (Topic Sentence)**: Introduces the main idea (the danger of human trials).
2. **Sentence A (Supporting Detail)**: Explains who participates and why (financial incentives).
3. **Sentence C (Explanation)**: Explains the danger of this incentive (agreeing to unhealthy procedures).
4. **Sentence B (Result)**: Illustrates the ultimate impact (developing serious illnesses).

*Logical Sequence: **D $\rightarrow$ A $\rightarrow$ C $\rightarrow$ B***

#### Model Task 2 Essay: Animal Testing of Medicines
*Prompt: Some people believe that it is wrong to test new medicines on animals. Do you think the advantages of testing new medicines on animals outweigh the disadvantages?*

##### Paragraph 1: Introduction
Many people think that using animals is the best way to find out if new medicines work. Others, however, disagree, saying that it is not right to make use of animals in this way. Let us consider both sides of the argument.

##### Paragraph 2: Disadvantages (Cruelty and Inaccuracy)
It is certainly true that there are disadvantages to using animals to test new medicines. Firstly, it is cruel. Is an animal’s life worth less than that of a human? Animals are living creatures too and we should respect them. Secondly, a new medicine may not work in the same way on an animal as it does on a person. So the conclusions the researchers come to may be wrong.

##### Paragraph 3: Advantages (Medical Advances and Human Safety)
However, it is very important to test new medicines. Scientists need to develop new medicines so that doctors can improve the quality of life for millions of people all over the world. Giving new medicines to animals will show that they work well. These experiments are also necessary to make sure that the new medicines pharmaceutical companies develop do not have any serious side effects. It is too risky to experiment with new drugs on humans but if chemists are not able to test them, then sick children and adults will not be able to benefit from new treatments.

##### Paragraph 4: Conclusion
I would therefore argue that although it is not good if animals suffer, the advantages of using them to test new medicines outweigh the disadvantages. It is very important for the future of medical science.

### 4. IELTS Speaking Practice: Health & Nature

#### Part 1: Health, Fitness & Environment
1. **Do you do any regular physical activity?**
   * *Band 7+ Model Answer*: Yes, absolutely. I make a conscious effort to go for a brisk thirty-minute walk every evening, and on weekends I usually go swimming or cycle with friends to stay in shape.
2. **Do you like spending time in natural places?**
   * *Band 7+ Model Answer*: Definitely. Whenever I feel overwhelmed with work, I like to visit local parks or botanical gardens. Being surrounded by greenery and fresh air helps me decompress and clears my mind.
3. **What do you do when you catch a cold?**
   * *Band 7+ Model Answer*: I generally prefer home remedies. I drink plenty of warm herbal tea with honey and ginger, make sure I get sufficient rest, and only take over-the-counter medication if I have a high fever.

#### Part 2: Long Turn (Cue Card)
> **Describe a healthy habit you have recently developed.**
>
> You should say:
> * What the habit is
> * How long you have had it
> * Why you decided to start it
> * And explain how it has benefited your overall well-being.

* *Band 8.0 Model Response*:
  > "I’d like to talk about my habit of meal prepping healthy, home-cooked lunches every Sunday. I started doing this about six months ago. Previously, I had a tendency to eat fast food or grab processed snacks during my office lunch break, which was making me feel sluggish and led to slight weight gain.
  >

#### Part 3: In-Depth Discussion (Healthcare & Ecological Preservation)
1. **Do you think governments should do more to promote physical fitness among young people?**
   * *Band 7.5+ Model Answer*: Yes, without a doubt. Sedentary lifestyles are becoming increasingly prevalent due to screen time and gaming. Governments ought to invest in public sports facilities, subsidized community sports clubs, and mandate comprehensive physical education in schools to instill lifelong fitness habits early on.
2. **Why is it essential to protect biodiversity and natural ecosystems?**
   * *Band 7.5+ Model Answer*: Biodiversity is fundamental not only for ecological equilibrium but also directly for human survival. As scientific research shows, over half of modern pharmaceuticals are derived from natural compounds found in rainforests and marine organisms. If we allow ecosystems to degrade, we permanently forfeit potential cures for life-threatening diseases.
