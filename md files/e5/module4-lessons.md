## Module 4a: Health

### 1. Reading: Understand Connections & Matching Sentence Endings

The primary focus of Module 4a Reading is **Matching Sentence Endings** and understanding **connections** within sentences (such as identifying conditions, causes, consequences, and contrasts).

#### Reading Passage: "Monitoring Fitness"
*Are fitness apps really work? Are they helpful? Or do they drown out the conversation people should be having with their bodies?*

##### Paragraph A
Technology has become an important aspect of life for people who want to get and stay fit. They use apps on their smartphone or other devices, for example, to count the number of steps they take every day, to measure their heart rate, to record their sleep patterns and to work out how many calories they consume.

##### Paragraph B
Sports scientists recognise that deciding to use a fitness tracker or app is good because it means a person is curious about health and keen to improve their habits. If the attractive design of a device or app encourages an interest in keeping fit, its impact is clearly positive. After all, many people have reported that fitness devices have motivated them to get off the sofa and go for a walk after dinner.

##### Paragraph C
Unfortunately, however, many of those after-dinner walkers have added that when their devices broke and they no longer knew how many steps they had taken, there seemed little point in heading out for that walk. Consequently, sports scientists agree that people need to be aware of the fact that apps alone are not enough.

##### Paragraph D
One of the problems with fitness trackers is that they usually measure very limited pieces of information – steps taken, movements when asleep or heart rate, for example – and this does not give a clear overall picture of a person’s health. They may record that someone worked out for two hours on a rowing machine, but not that they then spent the rest of the day eating pizza and watching DVDs. In other words, fitness apps can help people to deceive themselves about how healthy their lifestyle is.

##### Paragraph E
What sports psychologists recommend users of electronic fitness devices to do is raise their awareness of how their body looks. No one actually needs an app to tell them that they have had a bad night’s sleep; the bags under their eyes can do that just as well. And if people want to lose weight, they may find it more effective to take a regular look at themselves in the mirror rather than try to motivate themselves to diet by checking everything they eat against a calorie counting app. Unfortunately, tracking the calories is also ineffective because the body metabolises calories from sugar differently than those from fat.

##### Paragraph F
Keeping a fitness diary is also useful. In it, people should record what they ate and what their mood was two or three hours later. Tracking calories in a food app after eating a large tub of ice cream is not going to solve anyone’s problems. Reflecting on how bad they felt after eating it will. Keeping a fitness diary helps people to eat well and exercise not for the sake of meeting the app’s artificial targets, but because exercise makes everyone feel, look and be better.
*(Note: metabolise means to change food in the body into energy and new cells.)*

Match the sentence beginnings (1–5) with the correct endings (A–G) based on the passage:

##### Sentence Beginnings:
1. **Sports scientists approve of the decision to use fitness apps because...** $\rightarrow$ **D** *(it shows that the user wishes to develop better fitness practices)*
2. **People have reported that when their fitness apps stop working...** $\rightarrow$ **F** *(they lose the motivation to be active)*

* **G** *(they won’t help you achieve your goals)*

### 2. Language Development: Past Simple & Past Continuous

The past simple and past continuous are used to discuss actions and situations in the past.

#### Crucial Uses

1. **Past Simple**: Used to talk about actions, states, or situations that started and finished in the past. We often use it with specific past time expressions (e.g., *yesterday, last week, last year, three years ago, in 2018*).
   * *Regular Verb Form*: Verb + `-ed` (e.g., *started, exercising, increased, died*).
   * *Irregular Verbs*: (e.g., *go* $\rightarrow$ *went*, *lose* $\rightarrow$ *lost*, *do* $\rightarrow$ *did*, *have* $\rightarrow$ *had*, *be* $\rightarrow$ *was/were*).
2. **Past Continuous**: Used to describe:
   * **An action in progress at a specific time in the past**: *"At 8 o'clock this morning I was working out at the gym."*
   * **Two actions in progress at the same time in the past** (often with *while*): *"I was studying while my friends were enjoying themselves."*
   * **Background scene in a story**: *"The sun was just rising and it was starting to get warm."*
3. **Past Simple & Past Continuous Together**:
   * We use them together to show that a shorter, sudden action (Past Simple) interrupted a longer action already in progress (Past Continuous).
   * *Example*: *"I was waiting for the bus when it began to rain."*
   * *Example*: *"While I was swimming, somebody stole my purse."*
   * *Rule*: We use *when* before the past simple action and *while* before the past continuous action.
   * *Rule*: If actions happen consecutively (one after another), we use the Past Simple for both: *"I fell over and hurt my leg."*
   * *Rule*: Stative verbs are not used in the past continuous: *"I didn’t understand what he was saying."* (NOT *"I wasn't understanding..."*).

#### Form & Structure Matrix

| Sentence Type | Past Simple | Past Continuous |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Positive (+)** | Subject + past verb (regular/irregular)<br>- *I lost weight last year.* | Subject + `was/were` + Verb-ing<br>- *I was working out at 8 a.m.* |
| **Negative (-)** | Subject + `didn't` + base verb<br>- *Al didn't go to work yesterday.* | Subject + `wasn't/weren't` + Verb-ing<br>- *They weren't following a diet.* |
| **Question (?)** | `Did` + Subject + base verb?<br>- *Did you do any exercise last week?* | `Was/Were` + Subject + Verb-ing?<br>- *Were you trying to lose weight?* |

##### Exercise D: "Changes in lifestyle" Cloze Text

Over the years our ideas and habits regarding health can change dramatically. For example, in the middle of the **last** (1) century many people didn’t realise the dangers of smoking. By the 1960s scientists **were** (2) just starting to realise that smoking can affect health, but it **was** (3) only later that the public really began to understand the dangers.

It was only **when** (9) people started to do less active jobs and eat more sugar that they started to have weight problems. Therefore, it’s easy to see why people at that time **weren't** (10) always trying to lose weight like they are now and why they didn’t need to go to the gym.

### 3. Vocabulary: Illnesses, Symptoms, Injuries & Treatments

Developing precise vocabulary regarding healthcare, medical situations, and symptoms is essential for both the Listening and Speaking tests of the IELTS exam.

#### Illnesses and Symptoms
Match the symptom description to the medical noun:
1. **allergy**: A food reaction, runny nose, sore eyes, itchy skin, or rash.
2. **heart attack**: A chest pain, difficulty breathing, or cardiovascular event.
3. **stress**: Feeling tired, anxious, and worried due to modern lifestyle pressures.
4. **food poisoning**: A serious illness caused by bacteria in contaminated food.
5. **asthma**: A chronic condition causing breathing difficulties and coughing.
6. **infection**: An illness caused by bacteria or virus (e.g. chest or throat infection).
7. **flu**: A high temperature, aching body, sore throat, and fatigue.
8. **cold**: A runny nose, minor cough, and sneezing.

#### Accidents & Injuries (Verbal Collocations)
* Mosquito **bites** vs. Bee **stings**.
* **Break** a bone (e.g., *break a leg*) vs. **Sprain** a joint (e.g., *sprain an ankle*).
* **Burn** oneself (on hot surfaces/steam) vs. **Cut** oneself (on sharp objects/knives).
* **Bruise**: A painful purple mark on the skin after an impact.

#### Medical Treatments
Match the medical word to its definition:
* **prescription**: A piece of paper on which a doctor writes the medicine a patient should receive.
* **surgery**: A medical operation in which a doctor cuts open the body to repair or remove something.
* **antibiotics**: A medicine used to kill bacteria and treat infections.
* **vaccination**: An injection to prevent a disease before it occurs.
* **prevention**: Taking actions to stop an illness or accident from happening (*"Prevention is better than cure"*).
* **treatment**: Medical care given to a patient for an illness or injury.
* **cure**: A medicine or medical treatment that makes an illness go away completely.

#### Speaking Collocations: Staying Healthy
* Have a **balanced diet** (including meat, fruit, vegetables).
* Get **enough sleep** (to let the body recover).
* Go for **medical check-ups** regularly.
* Stay in / keep in **shape** (by exercising and eating well).
* Do / practise **regular exercise** (to maintain fitness).

### 4. Writing: Opinion Essays & Paraphrasing in introductions

#### The Role of Paraphrasing
When writing an introduction for IELTS Academic Writing Task 2, you must never copy the words of the prompt verbatim. Instead, you should paraphrase the prompt using synonyms, parallel structures, and different grammatical patterns.

#### Prompt Paraphrasing Matrix

| Prompt Word/Phrase | Paraphrased Alternative |
| --- | --- |
| **Schoolchildren of all ages** | Pupils aged from five to eighteen / Young people |
| **A sports lesson every day** | Physical education daily / A daily sports session |
| **Help improve their fitness** | Make them fitter and healthier |

#### Example Paraphrase Comparisons
* **Prompt**: *Some people believe that all children should have to do some kind of organised physical activity every day.*
  * **Strong Paraphrase**: *Many people argue that some form of physical exercise should be a daily part of every child’s life.* (Preserves the exact meaning).
  * **Weak Paraphrase**: *Some people believe that teachers need to give all children regular lessons in how to play sports well.* (Narrows the meaning incorrectly to playing sports).

---

#### Model Introduction: The Gym Essay
*Prompt: Some people choose to keep fit by joining a gym but gyms can be crowded and expensive. Do you think the advantages of joining a gym outweigh the disadvantages?*

> "Many people nowadays decide that taking out a gym membership will be an ideal way for them to stay healthy. However, belonging to a gym has both positive and negative aspects and although some people are happy with this decision, others quickly regret it. It is, therefore, sensible to consider what these aspects are before paying membership fees."

##### Structural Analysis of the Model Introduction:
1. **Sentence 1**: Introduces the general topic area in the writer's own words.
2. **Sentence 2**: Suggests how opinions on the topic may differ and acknowledges both sides (advantages and disadvantages).
3. **Sentence 3**: Thesis statement suggesting the approach the writer is going to take (analyzing both aspects before giving a final stance).

## Module 4b: Nature

### 1. Reading: Understand Connections & Matching Sentence Endings

The primary focus of Module 4b Reading is identifying connections between ideas across paragraphs and using matching sentence endings.

#### Reading Passage: "Remedies from Nature"

##### Paragraph A
Animal and plant species have given people important medicines such as quinine and aspirin, as well as many cancer and HIV-fighting drugs. People have used plants and animals as sources of medicine for thousands of years. For example, medicinal plants that people used over 60,000 years ago were found in an Iraqi cave site. A fur strap found on the arm of a 5,000 year-old Ice Man from the Alps contained a type of fungus which is able to kill bacteria.

##### Paragraph B
Most people are amazed to discover that our dependence on nature for health has not reduced. Over the past quarter century, more than half of all the products that drug companies have developed actually use, or copy, substances from the natural world. Moreover, the World Health Organization estimates that in many developing countries, 80 percent of the population relies on traditional medicines from natural sources.

##### Paragraph C
However, scientists generally believe that researchers have fully examined less than one percent of all species in order to discover their possible uses in medical treatments. They believe that nature still holds many valuable cures for research to discover. In particular, they point to the importance of tropical rainforests as a potential source of new medicines. Although rainforests cover only six percent of the earth’s land surface, they contain over half of its biodiversity.

##### Paragraph D
Unfortunately, the ecosystems that provide some of the world’s most important drugs, such as rainforests and coral reefs, are also the ecosystems that are most at risk today. There are concerns that warming temperatures mean that few coral reefs will remain by the end of the century. Meanwhile, agriculture and various major development projects are making the world’s rainforests vanish at an alarming rate – the world is losing more than 320 km2 every day. The inevitable result is that many species with important medicinal powers will become extinct.

##### Paragraph E
Another problem is that most of nature’s medicines do not come from big and beautiful mammals, such as tigers and elephants. Instead, they come from the least popular of the world’s ecosystems: plants, fungi and invertebrates. Some particularly valuable species are often either poisonous or so small that we cannot see them without a microscope. This makes campaigning for their preservation much more difficult.

##### Paragraph F
An additional problem is the fact that medical schools teach their students very little about the discovery of new treatments. Few young doctors, for example, know that the blood pressure medicine captopril, one of the best-selling drugs of all time, comes from the poison of a Brazilian snake. Antibiotics, most of which come from nature, are a miracle drug but doctors use far too many of them. This is partly because they do not realise the dangers involved or how closely connected these drugs are with the natural world.

##### Paragraph G
Nature and medicine are closely connected. We must protect nature. If we don’t, we not only risk losing many plant and animal species but we are also risking our own health. In all the discussions about saving the world’s biodiversity from extinction, people often forget one point: the world’s species provide people with a large number of life-saving medicines.
*(Note: fungus means a simple type of plant with no leaves or flowers; biodiversity means the variety of plants and animals; invertebrate means an animal without a backbone; preservation means keeping something in its original state.)*

### 2. Vocabulary: Wildlife, Ecosystems & Nature Verbs

Precise terminology regarding the natural world, conservation, and physical actions is essential for IELTS reading matching tasks and speaking.

#### Wildlife and Ecosystems Gap-fill
*Words: endangered, farm, fish, fur, habitat, hunt, ocean, protect, rainforest, species.*

Sadly, humans are destroying the natural **habitat** (1) of many animals. For example, humans cut down trees in the **rainforest** (2) so that they can use the land to **farm** (3) and produce more crops. This means that some **species** (4) of animals, birds and insects that live there are **endangered** (5) because they have nowhere to live. In addition, some people **hunt** (6) wild animals for their meat or **fur** (7), or even just for sport. Humans also produce a lot of rubbish, which often ends up in the **ocean** (8) and can affect the sea life, and pollution in rivers can kill all the **fish** (9). It is important that governments introduce policies to **protect** (10) the natural world and the wildlife that lives there.

#### Nature Verbs
*   Do not **pick** flowers from the wild (leave them where they are).
*   People should not be allowed to **fish** in protected lakes and rivers.
*   We must try to **protect** endangered plants and animals.
*   Governments must assist individuals who **farm** the land to do so sustainably.
*   **Hunting** wild animals for sport is cruel and should be forbidden.
*   Individuals should try to **grow** their own vegetables in their gardens.

### 3. Writing: Animal Testing Task 2 Essay (Organising Ideas & Topic Sentences)

#### Paragraph Coherence: Topic Sentences
A cohesive paragraph must begin with a **topic sentence** that introduces the main idea. All subsequent sentences must provide details, explanations, or results of that main idea.

##### Paragraph Coherence Exercise (Verbatim Scrambled Paragraph)
*   **Sentence A**: Poor people may decide to take part in a drugs experiment because pharmaceutical companies usually pay participants.
*   **Sentence B**: As a result, they may even become seriously ill themselves.
*   **Sentence C**: This may encourage them to agree to do things that they know are not good for their bodies.
*   **Sentence D**: Testing new medicines on people can have serious consequences.

##### Correct Analytical Order:
1.  **D (Topic Sentence)**: introduces the main danger of human testing.
2.  **A (Supporting Detail)**: explains why some vulnerable people participate (financial motivation).
3.  **C (Explanation)**: links the financial motivation to agreeing to dangerous procedures.
4.  **B (Final Result)**: details the ultimate physiological harm.

#### Model Essay: Animal Testing of Medicines
*Prompt: Some people believe that it is wrong to test new medicines on animals. Do you think the advantages of testing new medicines on animals outweigh the disadvantages?*

##### Paragraph 1: Introduction
Many people think that using animals is the best way to find out if new medicines work. Others, however, disagree, saying that it is not right to make use of animals in this way. Let us consider both sides of the argument.

##### Paragraph 2: Disadvantages (Animal Suffering and Inaccuracy)
It is certainly true that there are disadvantages to using animals to test new medicines. Firstly, it is cruel. Is an animal’s life worth less than that of a human? Animals are living creatures too and we should respect them. Secondly, a new medicine may not work in the same way on an animal as it does on a person. So the conclusions the researchers come to may be wrong.

##### Paragraph 3: Advantages (Medical Breakthroughs and Safety)
However, it is very important to test new medicines. Scientists need to develop new medicines so that doctors can improve the quality of life for millions of people all over the world. Giving new medicines to animals will show that they work well. These experiments are also necessary to make sure that the new medicines pharmaceutical companies develop do not have any serious side effects. It is too risky to experiment with new drugs on humans but if chemists are not able to test them, then sick children and adults will not be able to benefit from new treatments.

##### Paragraph 4: Conclusion (The Stance)
I would therefore argue that although it is not good if animals suffer, the advantages of using them to test new medicines outweigh the disadvantages. It is very important for the future of medical science.
