### 1. Reading: Identify Ideas & Opinions (Yes/No/Not Given)

The primary focus of Module 10a Reading is identifying the writer's ideas and opinions through **Yes/No/Not Given** questions. To solve these successfully, you must determine whether the statements agree with (Yes), contradict (No), or are unsupported by (Not Given) the reading passage.

#### Reading Passage: "Technology and Communication"

Advertisements for the latest smartphones, tablets, laptops and other gadgets say that they will make communication faster and more efficient [150]. Naturally enough, because new technology can do so many things so easily, people have come to depend on it [150]. But this has some unfortunate consequences [150].

Geoffrey Tumlin is the author of a book on the theme of communications in our contemporary world [150]. His basic argument – that people today give too much importance to gadgets rather than each other – will surprise many people but it is strong and clear [150]. He says the result of such dependency is that people feel dissatisfied with their experience of using these gadgets to communicate [150].

Electronic devices have certainly made the sending and receiving of messages much simpler than it was in the past, but there is more to communication than just hitting the ‘Send’ button or clicking to open a message [151]. Real communication does not happen until the other person understands the message, and people forget about this important stage in far too many cases [151]. Tumlin says that adding an extra step – considering whether or not the message is understood – can make a person a much more effective communicator [151]. His argument is that most people are focusing only on efficiency when they approach the majority of their exchanges [151]. They want to clear their inboxes and respond to new text or voice messages as soon as they come in [151]. Similarly, they are in the habit of getting face-to-face conversations over as quickly as possible so they can move on to the next thing [151]. They feel that they have so many communication tasks to deal with every day that they spend as little time on each one as they can and are satisfied just by crossing one more job off their to-do list [151].

However, meaningful and effective communication is possible only when the speaker or writer thinks about their goals [152]. ‘In contrast with average communicators, great communicators think carefully about what they would like to achieve rather than about what they would like to say,’ Tumlin says [152]. He makes the point that it can be very difficult for people to stop themselves from saying something, especially when they are feeling angry or upset [152]. By making it so easy for everyone to share their thoughts and feelings, technology has encouraged them to do so [152]. But these same emotional messages, unfortunately, are also the ones that stop them from achieving their communication goals [152].

One of the greatest problems with the digital age is that sending a message to lots of people is just as easy as sending a message to one person [153]. But having a conversation with someone much older or younger than you may be different from speaking to someone your age [153]. Similarly, a conversation with one friend is different from a discussion with 100 friends [153]. But because all people have to do is click to send an email to all of their contacts or to post a social media message for the world to see, they think, incorrectly, that adding people to a message does not require any extra thought [153]. Tumlin argues that communication gets much more difficult as the number of people receiving a message is increased [153]. Including more people means there are more points of view to take into account [153]. When people do not remember these additional viewpoints, it may mean that the other person ignores what was said or, more seriously perhaps, is upset by a thoughtless message [153]. So before someone sends a message to a group, he or she should ask questions like, ‘What’s my uncle going to think about this post?’ or ‘I wonder how this email is going to come across to the other departments in my workplace.’ [153] Adding people complicates communication and when that is forgotten, misunderstandings will follow [153].

### 2. Language Development: Defining Relative Clauses

Defining relative clauses give essential information about a person, thing, or place, making it clear exactly who or what we are referring to [194].

#### Relative Pronouns & Rules
1.  **who** or **that**: Used to refer to people [194].
    *   *Example*: "There are very few people **who** write letters these days." [194]
2.  **which** or **that**: Used to refer to things [194].
    *   *Example*: "I’d like to get a phone **which** has a good camera." [194]
3.  **whose**: Used to show possession [194].
    *   *Example*: "I think I know **whose** telephone number this is." [194]
4.  **where**: Used to refer to a place [194].
    *   *Example*: "There’s a café next door **where** you can get free wi-fi." [194]

#### Grammar Rule: Subject vs. Object Relative Pronouns
*   A relative pronoun can act as the **subject** or the **object** of a relative clause [195].
*   When the relative pronoun is the **object** of the relative clause, **we can omit (leave out) the pronoun** [195].
    *   *Subject clause (pronoun required)*: "There are a number of messaging apps **which** are free." [195] (Here, *which* is the subject of *are*).
    *   *Object clause (pronoun optional)*: "There’s a new messaging app **[which]** I want to get." [195] (Here, *I* is the subject, and *which* is the object of *want to get*).
*   **Note**: The relative pronoun **that** can replace *who* or *which*, but it **cannot** replace *whose* or *where* [195].

### 3. Vocabulary: Verbal Actions & Communication Adjectives

Developing a range of terms to describe spoken and non-verbal communication is vital for the Speaking and Writing modules.

#### Spoken and Non-Verbal Verbs & Collocations [160, 161]
*   **Inform** vs. **Announce**:
    *   *Inform*: To pass on necessary work/life information. "In the workplace, email is the best way to **inform** everybody about what's going on." [161]
    *   *Announce*: To state something officially/publicly. "I don't like using instant messaging to **announce** important family news." [191]
*   **Promise**: "If you **promise** to do something, you should always do it." [161]
*   **Repeat**: "Some people think that if they keep **repeating** the same point, you will believe them." [161]
*   **Look [someone] in the eye**: "You can't trust a person who doesn't **look** you in the eye when they're talking to you." [160]
*   **Nod**: Moving the head up and down to show agreement/attention. "It is a good idea to **nod** your head when someone is talking – it shows you are listening." [160]
*   **Shake**: Moving the head left to right. "If you **shake** your head, it can be a sign of sadness as well as meaning 'no'." [160]
*   **Raise / Lower [one's voice]**: "I only **raise** my voice when I'm angry." [160] / "If you want to talk in the library, you should always **lower** your voice." [160]
*   **Point**: "It's very rude to **point** your finger at someone." [160]

### 4. Writing: Opinion Essay (Face-to-Face vs. Technology)

#### Task 2 Prompt
*Nowadays it is no longer necessary to meet face to face in order to have a conversation. We can email, telephone, text message or share our news on a social network. However, many people argue that none of these modern means of communication is as good as talking to someone directly [164].*
*To what extent do you agree or disagree?* [164]

#### Unabridged Model Answer & Paragraph Breakdown [164, 165]

**Paragraph 1: Introduction (Paraphrase & Thesis)**
> Nowadays it is no longer necessary to meet face to face in order to have a conversation [164]. We can email, telephone, text message or share our news on a social network [164]. However, many people argue that none of these modern means of communication is as good as talking to someone directly [164].

**Paragraph 2: Body Paragraph 1 (Advantages of Modern Methods)**
> In my opinion, modern ways of communication have many advantages [164]. Firstly, the ability to text friends and family probably means that we communicate with them more often than in the past and this has to be a positive thing [164]. Technology like Skype means we can have close and satisfying contact with relatives in other countries [164]. Secondly, using social media means we can make connections with new people in other cultures and this must be good for improving international understanding [164].

**Paragraph 3: Body Paragraph 2 (Irreplaceable Benefits of Face-to-Face Contact)**
> However, it is true that face-to-face communication has many advantages too [165]. There is nothing better than spending a long evening with a friend chatting and laughing, as well as talking about more serious things [165]. You can tell a lot about how someone is feeling through their body language and their tone of voice, and you don’t get these from texts or social network posts [165]. Moreover, you can’t hug your mum or share a cup of tea with a friend when you’re on the phone or the laptop [165].

**Paragraph 4: Conclusion (Balanced Summary & Final Opinion)**
> In conclusion, I think that the different ways of communication we enjoy today are wonderful and have enriched people’s lives a great deal [165]. However, talking to someone face to face remains the best form of contact that we can have with another person [165].

#### Reading Passage: "Cultural Diversity in Business Today"

**[Paragraph A]**
Many businesses today employ people from a range of different cultures [177]. This means that communication issues may sometimes cause problems [177]. Therefore, companies need to do all they can to make sure that managers and employees are communicating effectively [177]. Business success depends on good communications between a company and its staff [177].

**[Paragraph D]**
However, there are other less obvious cultural differences that affect communication [178]. There is the issue of personal space, for example [178]. An American is used to being about 1.5 metres away from the other person when talking to them [178]. Germans and the Japanese may find that distance uncomfortably close while Arabs and Latin Americans may feel that that is too far apart to be friendly [178]. The amount of eye contact that people make with one another is also different in different cultures [178]. Europeans and North Americans feel that eye contact is necessary and shows attention and honesty, while in some Asian and Latin cultures too much eye contact can suggest a lack of respect [178]. Greeting people can also vary a great deal between cultures [178]. Some cultures typically shake hands when they meet, others give each other a kiss and others simply say hello [178].

**[Paragraph E]**
Sociologists divide cultures into two types, which they call ‘high-context’ and ‘low-context’ [179]. High-context cultures are those that give a lot of importance to nonverbal communication, actions and contexts [179]. In other words, body language, behaviour and relationships communicate almost as powerfully as words [179]. Japan, India and the Middle East are examples of high-context cultures [179]. Low-context cultures, on the other hand, emphasise the importance of language for communication and do not pay as much attention to other information [179]. Examples of low-context cultures are the US, the UK and Germany [179]. Neither type, of course, is better than the other; the important thing is simply to be aware of the difference in values [179].

**[Paragraph F]**
It is also important to remember that cultural differences relating to roles and status can also have an impact on communication in the workplace [180]. For instance, different attitudes to the roles and status of men and women can affect how a male employee feels about having a female manager or how comfortable a female employee feels when a male colleague talks to her [180]. Moreover, some cultures accept quite informal relationships between people with a different status in an organisation or between people of different ages, while others feel that younger people or those in a lower position should treat their managers or their elders in a formal, respectful way [180].

**[Paragraph H]**
If a business values the cultural diversity of its staff and encourages them all to understand and respect one another, the company will gain many advantages [181]. It will not only be more productive but it will also be a happier place for all the people who work there [181].

### 4. Writing: Opinion Essay (Globalisation & Cultural Traditions)

#### Task 2 Prompt
*It is almost impossible to keep our traditions in a global community [181].*
*To what extent do you agree or disagree?* [181]

#### Unabridged Model Answer & Linking Markers [181, 182]

**Paragraph A: Introduction**
> It is undoubtedly true that nowadays more and more people are travelling round the world [181]. They do this not just for holidays, but also to live and work abroad [181]. Some people are afraid that this means we will all lose our unique cultures and traditions [181].

**Paragraph B: Body Paragraph 1 (Why traditions might be threatened)**
> On the one hand, global business and modern media have clearly made different countries more similar than in the past. Global fast-food chains, clothing brands, and entertainment products are available in almost every major city on Earth. Consequently, younger generations are increasingly adopting global consumer habits instead of following local traditions, which has led to concerns that traditional ways of life may eventually disappear.

**Paragraph C: Body Paragraph 2 (Why globalisation strengthens local identity)**
> However, on the other hand, the global community can also have a positive impact on preserving heritage. For instance, the internet and social media allow small communities to document and share their unique cultural customs with a global audience. This access can spark a renewed pride in local traditions, encouraging younger people to learn their ancestral languages or protect historical architecture. Thus, international contact often highlights the value of diversity rather than destroying it.

**Paragraph D: Conclusion**
> To conclude, it is certainly true that many people these days are moving away from their original communities and there is much more contact between people of different cultures than ever before [182]. However, I believe that this may actually be making many traditions stronger rather than weaker [182].
