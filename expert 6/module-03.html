🎯 Summary Scanning Rules

🟢 Anchor First Sentences: Read the first sentence of each paragraph to identify its core topic before reading summary gaps.

1. Syllable & Ending Rules
  • 1 Syllable: Add -er / -est (low → lower, lowest).
  • 2 Syllables in -y: Replace -y with -ier (easy → easier, heavy → heavier).
  • 3+ Syllables / Adverbs in -ly: Use more / less (accurately → more accurately, common → less common).
2. Modifiers & Proportions
• Big differences: significantly, considerably, far, much, nowhere near as... as
• Small differences: slightly, marginally, a bit, almost as... as
• Multiples: twice as high as (not twice higher), three/four times higher than / as high as.

📊 Band 6 Coherence & Cohesion

  • Uses cohesive devices effectively: connect and contrast ideas clearly.
  • Avoid faulty cohesion: do not combine two subordinators (Although... but).
  • Prepositional vs Conjunction: despite / in spite of + noun/gerund; although + full clause.
Prompt: The graph shows the average amount of money women earned as a percentage of men's earnings in the USA from 1975 to 2005. Write at least 150 words.
Introduction & Overview

The chart gives an overview of the gap between male and female earnings in the USA over a 30-year period, starting in 1975. The chart shows that men consistently earned more than women throughout that period, but the gap was greatest among those of the older age groups.

Detailed Comparisons

In 1975, even the youngest age group, 16–24 years, were earning around 78% of male wages, while the oldest group, 45–54 years, earned almost half that of their male counterparts, at just 58%. Over the decades, all age groups showed a steady improvement, with the exception of women aged 35–44 years, which stopped improving after 1995. In fact, they were the least well-off by the end of the period. While the gap between men's and women's earnings closed, men still earned more than women.

Conclusion

From the chart, we can conclude that although the pay gap decreased, certain age groups do appear to be more disadvantaged than others.

📈 Band 7.5+ Model Moves

  • Data Selection: Contrasted youngest (78% ➔ 92%) with oldest (58% ➔ 75%).
  • Highlight Anomaly: 35–44 plateauing after 1995 at 73%.
  • Reference Devices: "those of older groups", "half that of their counterparts".
  • Sophisticated Conjunctions: "while", "with the exception of", "In fact", "although".
