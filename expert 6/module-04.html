RECOGNISING SCIENTIFIC ACHIEVEMENT

[Para 1] There are countless awards and prizes in science. Many institutes offer their own national awards, and then there are international prizes too. Probably the most famous of all of these are the annual Nobel Prizes. Established in his will, the awards were the creation of Swedish chemist Alfred Nobel. They have recognised significant achievements in disciplines including chemistry, physics and medicine since 1895. Each prize can be given to more than one scientist, although one cannot be offered to teams of more than three. Winners attend a formal dinner and receive a gold medal, a diploma and a sum of money, which could be as much as $1 million.

[Para 2] The UNESCO Niels Bohr Medal was first given 100 years after the birth of quantum physicist Niels Bohr to commemorate his contribution to science. Unlike the Nobel Prize, it is not an annual award. The medal has been given out a dozen times since 1985 to people whose research in physics has or could make a significant impact on the world. In 2010, three different researchers were given the medal for their outstanding work.

[Para 3] The world's oldest surviving prize for science is the UK's Royal Society's Copley Medal. It is not only for physicists. It is given each year to any researchers who have made a significant achievement in an area of science. It was first given in 1731, 170 years before the first Nobel Prize was won. Today it consists of a silver medal and £5000 but the original prize was the interest on £100 donated by wealthy landowner Sir Godfrey Copley.

[Para 4] Finally, the Ig Nobel prizes aim to recognise research which first makes us smile and then makes us think. While their research may not change the world, the researchers' unusual experiments offer an insight into how something — often small and sometimes unimportant — works. Every year the prizes are given out at a humorous award ceremony. Only Sir Andre Geim has won both the Ig Nobel Prize and Nobel Prize. He won the former for his research into using magnets to raise a frog into the air. He won the latter with his later work on the discovery of graphene, a form of carbon.

Answer: TRUE — Alfred Nobel is explicitly defined as a Swedish chemist in Paragraph 1.

SCIENTISTS AWARDED IG NOBEL PRIZES FOR UNUSUAL RESEARCH

[Para 1] A scientist who let bees sting him repeatedly on twenty-five different places on his body has been awarded the Ig Nobel Prize for physiology. The prize, which is now in its twenty-fifth year, is given for research that first makes you laugh, and then makes you think. Michael Smith, of Cornell University, was stung several times a day in different places on his body to find out which parts are the most sensitive. After weeks of research he found that many bee and wasp stings were uncomfortable, and the worst places to be stung were the upper lip and nostril, with the latter being the most painful.

[Para 2] A second researcher who won the physiology prize was Dr Justin Schmidt, a researcher from the Southwestern Biological Institute. He created the Schmidt Sting Pain Index which rates pain caused by bees, wasps and ants. Schmidt believes he's been stung more than a thousand times by 150 different species. While a honey bee only provides a level-two sting, a bullet ant causes the most pain at level four.

[Para 3] A third prize, given to scientists at Oxford University, did not involve animals. The scientists learnt that doctors can diagnose appendicitis — a serious medical condition — by the levels of pain the patient feels when travelling over speed bumps. Dr Helen Ashdown of the University of Oxford said: 'It may sound odd, but asking patients whether their pain worsened going over speed bumps on their way to hospital could help doctors in a diagnosis.' Once doctors have used this method to determine what the problem is, they can give the patient the correct medical treatment more quickly.

[Para 4] Finally, perhaps the most unusual prize of the night was the Biology Prize. It went to scientists from the University of Chile who attached a weighted stick to the back end of a chicken. The stick, which shared similar characteristics to a tail, resulted in the bird walking in a similar manner to a dinosaur.

📝 Exam Task Prompt

"In some countries, the number of university students studying science is decreasing. Some people believe the government should encourage more people to take science courses. Other people say that it is private industry's responsibility. Discuss both these views and give your own opinion."
Task Type: Discuss Both Views + Opinion

🔍 3 Core Prompt Questions

1. Impact of fewer science graduates: Economic stagnation, lack of innovation, medical shortages, and companies outsourcing overseas.
2. Reasons for Government Intervention: Preserving national economic health, public welfare, school curricula funding, and state infrastructure.
3. Reasons for Private Industry Responsibility: Private firms directly profit from innovations; they should fund scholarships, internships, and competitive wages.
🌟 Paragraph A

"The government should take action about the decreasing number of people choosing to study science as it is likely to have a negative effect on the country's economy. Many organisations need people who have an understanding of chemistry, physics and biology. If they are unable to find qualified staff, they may move their business to a country where scientists are available. To prevent the country's economy from suffering as a result of this, the government must ensure that a sufficient number of students study science at school."

⚠️ Paragraph B

"People need to learn science because otherwise there will be no one to do jobs in research or in science businesses like pharmaceutical companies. It is important for people to do science so we can learn new things and make new inventions. Companies need to employ new staff so we can have new things and new inventions. We have to train people to do this kind of work and the government can help with this. The government sometimes provides money to encourage people to study science at school."

Which paragraph is more effective for IELTS Band 7.5+?
Answer: Paragraph A — It has a clear topic sentence, logical expansion, sophisticated academic vocabulary (sufficient, qualified staff, suffering), and avoids Paragraph B's repetitive phrasing ("new things and new inventions").
1. Sentence 1: "The government should take action about the decreasing number of people choosing to study science as it is likely to have a negative effect on the country's economy."
Purpose B: Topic Sentence — States the core claim (government action required) and the overarching rationale (economic protection).
2. Sentences 2–3: "Many organisations need people who have an understanding of chemistry, physics and biology. If they are unable to find qualified staff, they may move their business to a country where scientists are available."
Purpose C: Supporting Development — Details organizational dependencies and provides conditional consequence (moving overseas).
3. Sentence 4: "To prevent the country's economy from suffering as a result of this, the government must ensure that a sufficient number of students study science at school."
Purpose A: Concluding Sentence — Synthesizes the argument into an actionable imperative.

2. Cause & Effect Connectors

Which connector signals the effect / result ?

4-Step Paragraph Checklist

1. Topic Sentence: Clear viewpoint + reason.
2. Explanation / Support: Provide concrete rationale.
3. Cohesive Glue: Use they, this, therefore, as a result.
4. Concluding Sentence: Summarize or deduce outcome.

🎬 Interstellar Astrophysics ⏳ Time Dilation Mechanics 🕳️ Wormholes vs Black Holes 🎯 Matching Features & Sentence Completion 🌌 Task 2 Space Exploration Essay

🎬 Cinema Science Reliability

1
How much should we believe facts in films? While directors consult astrophysicists (like Kip Thorne for Interstellar), plot requirements and visual spectacle often supersede scientific rigor.

🚀 Theoretical Feasibility

• Travel back in time: Physically impossible based on causality paradoxes.
• Travel forward in time: PROVEN FACT! High speed or intense gravity slows time relative to observers.
• Rapid transit across universes: Pure speculative fiction / multiverses.
• Speed of light travel: Impossible for mass (requires infinite energy).

1. Matching Features Protocol

Scan the text for the capitalized feature categories (Wormhole, Black Hole, Crop Blight, People Ageing). Statements do not follow passage order. Remember: you can use any letter more than once!

2. Watch for Qualifying Adverbs

Look for words that qualify scale or certainty (e.g. unlikely to happen on a large scale → only at a local level; small way → just a few microseconds).

3. Strict Word Count Constraint

Extract NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS directly from the passage. Do not alter verb tenses or plural endings. Ensure grammatical agreement with surrounding text.

THE SCIENCE OF INTERSTELLAR: FACT OR FICTION?

[Para 1] The film Interstellar is an enjoyable space adventure which sees Matthew McConaughey do a number of incredible things in space in order to save humanity. Director Christopher Nolan wanted to get details right and got advice from well-known astrophysicist Professor Kip Thorne. So is the film more science fact than science fiction? Well, not exactly. As Thorne himself says: 'Some of the science is known to be true, some is an educated guess, and some is speculation.' In his new book The Science of Interstellar he describes how much of the film is accurate.

[Para 2] One of the main themes in Interstellar is that characters can age at different speeds depending on where they are in the universe. When two characters travel to a distant planet, they age by only a few hours, but return to find their shipmate is 26 years older. Could that be true? Well yes, we have evidence of this. On Earth the effect is small, adding just a few microseconds a day to the time of space. However, it means that time is moving ever so slightly more quickly on the 10th floor compared to a basement. As a result, GPS satellites circling the Earth need to be changed as they are moving through time slightly more quickly than a person with a Sat Nav on Earth, although only by forty microseconds a day.

[Para 3] The main idea behind Interstellar is that the Earth is dying. Mankind needs to find a new home before the last crop — corn — is entirely used up, or is killed by the same disease that killed all the other crops. It is true that most people today do not grow their own food and depend on a global system of production. It is quite possible that the system could fail but this would be only at a local level. In addition, deadly diseases usually only attack one group of plants, not multiple groups, and diseases which affect more than one plant species are generally not that deadly. Also, the film doesn't talk about the fact that humans eat a lot of other things, like animals and fish.

Comprehension Check (Paras 1–3)

1. Who provided scientific advice to Christopher Nolan?
Answer: Professor Kip Thorne, renowned theoretical astrophysicist.
2. Where does time move faster on Earth?
Answer: 10th floor — Greater distance from Earth's gravitational center accelerates time.
3. Why is total global crop failure unlikely?
Answer: Biological pathogens specialize in single plant species.

THE SCIENCE OF INTERSTELLAR: FACT OR FICTION?

[Para 4] In Interstellar, the crew travelled large distances in space by jumping through a wormhole. A wormhole is a connection between two areas of space. Essentially, it's a kind of tunnel that allows you to travel quickly from one part of space to another. The term wormhole originally comes from a wormhole in an apple and it is the same kind of idea in space. An ant can reach the other side of an apple much faster if it goes through a hole in the centre. Wormholes can exist in theory but nobody knows how they could stay open long enough for someone to travel through them. Therefore they will probably never exist naturally in the universe. In Interstellar, the main character believes the wormhole was put there by an advanced group of people. However there is little chance that humans will find a way to control space and time in this way.

[Para 5] Finally, the crew of Interstellar's Endurance spaceship faced a problem when trying to get to a planet because it is under the control of a huge black hole. A black hole is an area of space that sucks everything around it inside. It's so strong that nothing can escape once it is inside the black hole. To stop the black hole pulling it in, the spaceship needed to travel at super-high speed. In the film, the main character solves this problem by travelling around the black hole and using that speed to get to the planet. This is not as strange as it sounds as something like this has been done in real life. It did not involve a black hole, but it did involve a space mission travelling around Earth and Mars to get enough speed to chase a comet. However, to escape a black hole, you would need a huge amount of speed. You also need to slow down again quickly to land on the planet, and the ship would almost certainly fall apart.

Physical Concepts Check (Paras 4–5)

4. What is the main practical problem with wormholes?
Answer: Wormholes collapse almost instantaneously in theoretical physics.
5. What real space mission used gravitational slingshots?
Answer: Gravitational slingshots around planets were used to chase comets (Rosetta mission).

🌌 Space Exploration vs Poverty ⚖️ 4-Stage Essay Architecture ✂️ Cohesion Upgrade Workshop 🏆 Band 8.5 Model Essay
🚀
Lead-in (Ex 1): Look at astronauts venturing into the cosmos. Why have humans travelled into space? Curiosity, national prestige, or survival?

Exam Prompt (p. 68)

"Some people believe that space travel is a waste of government money while others believe it is important for human development. Discuss both these views and give your opinion. Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience. Write at least 250 words."
Mandatory: 3 Invariable Elements

Mandatory Content Triad

1. View 1: Thoroughly explain why some consider space travel an irresponsible waste of tax revenues.
2. View 2: Articulate the counter-argument that aerospace research drives human and technological progress.
3. Clear Stance: Provide your own opinion from Introduction through Conclusion.

💸 View 1: Waste of Public Funds

  • Immediate Terrestrial Crises: Extreme poverty, lack of clean water, failing public schools, and underfunded healthcare systems.
  • Environmental Imperative: Global warming, clean energy transitions, and ocean decontamination require urgent multi-trillion funding.
  • Questionable Return on Investment: Billions spent sending probes into deep space offer no immediate relief to struggling citizens.

🚀 View 2: Crucial for Human Progress

  • Technological Spinoffs: Satellite telecommunications, GPS weather forecasting, advanced prosthetic materials, and CT/MRI sensors.
  • Resource & Energy Expansion: Asteroid mining for rare earth elements and extraterrestrial solar power capture.
  • Species Longevity: Safeguarding against planetary asteroid impacts and overpopulation.

Paraphrase background debate + state balanced thesis and clear personal stance.
~45 words

❌ Unedited Draft (Disjointed)

"Governments should not spend money on expensive journeys into space. There is the need for greater medical care. Millions of people around the world are unable to receive this kind of health care. They do not have enough money. Healthcare is just one of the things that we spend money on. ✅ Polished Version (Band 8.0 Flow)

"Governments should not spend money on expensive journeys into space. One reason for this is the need for greater medical care, because millions of people around the world are unable to receive this kind of health care as they do not have enough money. If the government cut their spending on space travel, they could provide free medical facilities for people who need it. This is likely to result in saving thousands of lives each year."

🛠️ 4 Steps Applied: (1) Cut irrelevant sentence • (2) Introduced causal link ("One reason for this is...") • (3) Subordinated clauses using "because" • (4) Added speculative impact sentence starting with "This is likely to result in...".

Sentence Transition Prompt

" there is a possibility that space exploration will help to discover new cures for medical conditions on Earth."
Why are these two options valid? Both However and On the other hand signal an adversarial rhetorical shift, preparing the examiner for the opposing perspective.

🔬 Language of Possibility Rationale

Why use "there is a possibility that..."?
In academic writing, speculative future outcomes must be hedged. Claiming that space exploration "will definitely cure diseases" is scientifically unsubstantiated and penalized under Task Achievement!
Paragraph 1: Introduction

While some commentators argue that space exploration represents an irresponsible squandering of public treasuries, proponents assert that astronomical missions are indispensable to the long-term advancement of humanity. In my opinion, although addressing acute socioeconomic crises on Earth must remain the primary duty of governments, aerospace research yields profound technological benefits that cannot be entirely dismissed.

Paragraph 2: View 1 (Terrestrial Allocation)

On the one hand, critics contend that spending billions on interplanetary exploration is unjustifiable when humanity faces immediate perils on our own planet. Millions of citizens continue to live in extreme poverty without access to clean water, reliable healthcare, or quality education. Furthermore, the accelerating threat of climate change demands trillions of dollars in renewable energy investments. Therefore, diverting massive state revenues toward distant celestial bodies while basic societal welfare remains underfunded is viewed by many as morally negligent.

Paragraph 3: View 2 (Technological Spinoffs)

On the other hand, advocates emphasize that space exploration serves as a formidable catalyst for technological innovation that directly enriches life on Earth. Revolutionary developments in satellite telecommunications, environmental weather tracking, and life-saving medical imaging originally stem from aerospace research. Moreover, developing the capacity to monitor asteroids and explore extraterrestrial mineral reserves may eventually secure the survival of our species. It is for this reason that curtailing space exploration entirely could stunt scientific progress.

Paragraph 4: Conclusion

In conclusion, both sides of the debate present compelling arguments. While the imperative to alleviate pressing domestic crises on Earth must rightfully receive the majority of national budgets, the long-term scientific dividends of space exploration are vital. Governments should therefore maintain modest investments in space science, perhaps through international consortiums and public-private ventures, while dedicating the lion's share of public funds to terrestrial welfare.

📝 Band 7.5+ Writing Checklist
  • Task Achievement: Are both perspectives discussed fully and supported with examples?
  • Clear Position: Is your personal opinion stated in the intro, maintained in body, and affirmed in conclusion?
  • Paragraph Architecture: Does every body paragraph start with a clear topic sentence and develop logically?
  • Coherence & Cohesion: Are reference pronouns (they, this, these) and connectors (therefore, as a result) accurate?
  • Word Count: Is the essay strictly at or above 250 words without irrelevant padding?

Repetitive vocabulary? Vary expressions: space travel → aerospace research → astronomical missions.
