1. Present Simple vs. Continuous
  • Present Simple: Regular habits, permanent states, general scientific truths ("Digital cameras scan images differently").
  • Present Continuous: Temporary situations, actions happening right now, trends ("Times are changing fast").
2. State Verbs (Non-Continuous)
Verbs of perception, cognition, and possession are never used in continuous forms:
• agree, believe, know, understand, prefer, want, need, belong, own, include.

📌 Writing Task 1 Prompt

The graph below shows the amount of income from cinema ticket and DVD sales in the USA and internationally from 2001 to 2010. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant. (Write at least 150 words).

📊 Preposition Accuracy Check

• Starting point: stood at $11.5 billion.
• Margin of change: rose by $6.5 billion.
• Ending point: finished at / reached [no prep] $22 billion.

🎯 Macro Contrast & Trends

International cinema revenue overtook all other categories, surging from $11bn to over $32bn, becoming twice as large as domestic US sales.

📝 Band 7+ Model Answer

The chart gives an overview of the changes in cinema and DVD sales in the United States and the rest of the world over a ten-year period from 2001 to 2010.

Overview: From the chart, we can tell that overall, DVD sales have been higher than cinema visits, and the international market has become significantly more important than the US market over the period.

At the beginning of the period, just under half of the US income came from cinema sales compared to DVDs, at $10 billion and $20 billion, respectively. Despite a slight rise in DVD sales around 2005, they finished in more or less the same position, at around $18 billion and $11 billion by 2010.

However, there were much bigger changes in international sales, with sales of DVDs rising from $11 billion to $22 billion by 2010. International cinema sales also experienced rapid growth, finishing as the largest market segment worth over $32 billion. This was more than twice as much as domestic sales, demonstrating how crucial overseas markets became.
