1. Paragraph A Context: "This" & "These"
"We love to reminisce and tell others about our extraordinary experiences – that time we climbed Mount Kilimanjaro, got to taste that rare food or ran into a celebrity on the street – but new research suggests this may not be such a wise idea. In fact, findings suggest that sharing these extraordinary experiences can come at a social cost."
What does "this" refer back to?
Answer: Sharing extraordinary experiences — "this" replaces the whole preceding action (telling others about rare events), while "these" points to the specific examples (climbing Kilimanjaro, rare food, celebrity).
2. Paragraph E Context: "His" & "He"
"Cooney, who conducted the research with co-authors Daniel T. Gilbert of Harvard University and Timothy D. Wilson of the University of Virginia, was interested in exploring the negative consequences of extraordinary experiences based on his own observations with others. He had noticed that we are keen to tell our friends..."
Who does "his" point to?
Answer: Gus Cooney — "his" and "He" are anaphoric pronouns referring back to the main subject of the sentence, Gus Cooney.
3. Academic Cohesion: "The former" vs "The latter"
"Researchers compared [1] ordinary daily topics with [2] extraordinary events; the former foster social bonding, whereas the latter often isolate the speaker."
What does "the former" refer to?
Answer: Ordinary daily topics — The former always refers back to the first mentioned option, while The latter refers to the second mentioned option.

💡 IELTS Cohesion & Reference Rules

• Anaphoric (Backward): Pronouns (this, these, his, its, former) replace previously stated nouns to avoid repetition.
• Whole-Clause Reference: Singular "this" often summarizes an entire previous sentence or complex concept rather than just a single word.
• Tracking Reference Chains: Always look at the immediately preceding clause to identify the exact noun phrase being replaced.
1. Purpose & Identification

Defining relative clauses identify, specify, or classify a noun. Without them, the sentence is incomplete.

👉 "Happiness is an emotion which makes us feel good."
2. Relative Pronoun Reference Chart
  • People: who / that
  • Things: which / that
  • Possessions: whose
  • Places & Times: where / when

⚡ Subject vs. Object Pronoun Rules

• Subject (Never Omit): "A person who is stressed has high blood pressure." (❌ Do not say: *who he is stressed*).
• Object (Can Omit): "Embarrassment is a feeling (that) we don’t want to experience."

🎓 Academic Formality Note

In formal IELTS writing, prefer which (for things) and who (for people) over that.

1. It is the machine lets you take money out of the bank.
2. Most things do seem easier you’re happier and less stressed.
3. This morning I noticed the plum trees are starting to bloom.
4. Look carefully at the smile of someone you’re close to.
5. Visit websites you can see amazing photos of nature.
6. People hear about the research might start to make an effort.

🔍 Pronoun Omission Rule (Ex 1b)

In Sentence 4, who/that refers to the object of a preposition ("close to [someone]"), meaning the relative pronoun can be omitted: "someone you're close to".

💡 Techniques 1–3
1. Cubing
Examine the topic from 6 angles: What, who, when, where, why, how (or describe, compare, evaluate, associate, argue).
2. Your perspective
Relate the prompt to your life (e.g. when money bought basic security vs. when friends brought true joy).
3. Other perspectives
Contrast different groups: wealthy vs. poor; young job-seekers vs. elderly retirees; urban vs. rural.
💡 Techniques 4–6 & Key Terms
4. Identify examples
Specific cases (e.g. paying for vital medical surgery vs. high-pressure executive burnout).
5. Flip a problem
Invert the premise: "Does having zero money create happiness?" → Reveals basic security is vital.
6. Generate vocabulary
financial stability, fundamental necessities, emotional well-being, intrinsic fulfillment.

Task 2 Prompt: Some people believe that money is one of the most important factors in a person's happiness. To what extent do you agree with this?

📌 Thesis Stance

Partially agree: Wealth provides essential security and eliminates poverty stress, but emotional bonds and health are far more vital for long-term fulfillment.

It is often argued that wealth plays a fundamental role in determining human happiness. In my view, while financial security is essential for meeting basic physical needs, it is not the sole or even the primary factor in achieving genuine contentment.

On the one hand, money is indispensable because it provides access to fundamental living standards. Without adequate income, individuals experience continuous anxiety regarding shelter, nutrition, and medical treatment. For example, families who are unable to afford quality education or healthcare for their children face enormous stress, which inevitably damages their quality of life. Furthermore, having sufficient financial resources affords people the freedom to pursue leisure activities and travel, which can enhance daily satisfaction.

On the other hand, non-material factors are far more influential once basic comfort has been secured. Strong interpersonal relationships with family and friends provide emotional support and a sense of belonging that wealth cannot purchase. In fact, many wealthy individuals suffer from acute loneliness or depression because of extreme work pressure. Moreover, good physical health and a fulfilling sense of purpose in life are critical components of well-being that exist independently of one's bank balance.

In conclusion, while money is a crucial foundation that prevents hardship and provides essential security, it is not the most important determinant of happiness. Deep social relationships, good health, and personal fulfillment ultimately matter far more in leading a joyful life.

🌟 Band 7+ Key Strengths

  • Balanced Nuance: Explicitly addresses "to what extent" by establishing the threshold between basic survival and emotional well-being.
  • Cohesion & Signposting: Natural transition phrases ("On the one hand", "On the other hand", "Furthermore", "In conclusion").
  • Grammatical Range: Accurate use of relative clauses ("families who are unable to afford...", "activities which can enhance...").

Task 2 Prompt: Both men and women care more about their appearance today than they did in the past. To what extent do you agree or disagree with this statement?

📌 Thesis Stance

Disagrees with the premise of both changing equally: argues female concern stayed high while male concern sharply increased.

For centuries, people have taken care of the way they dress. However, traditionally it has been women who are more concerned with their appearance than men. This essay will argue that women’s attitudes to appearance have not changed in recent decades, whereas men have become more concerned with how they look.

One reason for this is the growing importance of skin care. In the past, only women cared about their skin. They were the ones who put on cream each day to keep their faces looking young and wrinkle-free. Men used shaving products but nothing more. Today, they feel the pressure to have soft and young-looking skin too, and so there are now skin-care products especially for men, which are very popular among the younger generation.

Another reason for men caring more about their appearance is the role of the male celebrity. In the past, female celebrities were often famous because of their looks, but male celebrities were people who were clever or skilled. Their appearance was less important than their actions, but today it is often the opposite. Men see male celebrities and feel that they should try to look as good.

In conclusion, society has always felt that women should look attractive and, while body image is an issue today, this has not necessarily changed. Men, however, are required to pay much more attention to their appearance these days so they have good skin and look as good as celebrity role models. Appearance is now equally important for both men and women.
