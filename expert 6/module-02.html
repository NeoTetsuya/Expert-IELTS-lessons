 Test Practice • YES / NO / NOT GIVEN Critical Response: "Feeling Good" & Cooney's Study

Critical Response Passage

There are two main reasons why you might not agree with Cooney, Gilbert and Wilson’s research conclusions, and these do not include the fact that sixty-eight people is not a very high number to include in a study.

Firstly, the video of a street magician is not the same as an incredible life experience. The research uses cost-benefit analysis, i.e. the benefit of watching the video minus the cost of not being included socially. But it is unlikely that a street performer, even an amazing one, produces results similar to the feeling of an extraordinary experience. So, when the cost of not being included is taken away from the benefit of watching the video, of course the result will be more negative.

Secondly, one wonders why the researchers assume that people who do extraordinary things only spend time with boring people who have no bucket list themselves? In reality, if you explore the Costa Rican rainforest, maybe your friend goes sailing, gets an amazing professional qualification, or watches his child graduate from college. All of these can be considered great topics of conversation. Of course people will always have lots of everyday experiences to talk about too. After all, when you return from an inspiring trip, you still have to do the laundry and call your parents.

In addition, if you want to do fantastic things and not be lonely, you should choose friends who have similar ambitions.

🎯 1. Purpose & Pronoun Selection

• Identify & Classify: We use defining relative clauses to tell us exactly which person or thing is meant.
• People: who / that  |  Things: which / that
• Possession: whose  |  Places & Time: where / when

⚡ 2. Subject vs. Object Omission Rule

• Subject Pronoun (Cannot Omit): When the pronoun is followed by a verb ("A person who is stressed..."). Never add a duplicate subject ("who he is" ❌).
• Object Pronoun (Can Omit): When the pronoun is followed by a subject + verb ("Embarrassment is a feeling (that) we don't want...").

📋 Relative Pronouns in Academic English

Pronoun Usage Example
which Things (formal) a chemical which aids focus
who People students who study daily
whose Possessions a person whose work is valued
where Places environments where stress occurs
when Times moments when we feel relaxed

Task Prompt

"Our relationships with work colleagues or classmates are as important to our happiness as our relationship with our family. To what extent do you agree or disagree?"

Detailed Essay Plan
• Intro: Paraphrase prompt + clear thesis (family is fundamental).
• Body 1: Significance of family (unconditional support, permanence).
• Body 2: Role of colleagues/peers (shared interests, day-to-day goals).
• Conclusion: Restate that family remains the foundation.
Band 6+ Official Model Answer

The statement argues that relationships with our colleagues or fellow students have as much impact on our happiness as relationships with our own family. My view is that both types of relationship are extremely important but that, ultimately, family relationships are more significant.

I think that family relationships are key to our happiness because family ties are unbreakable. My sister, for example, moved to the other side of the world and has lost touch with friends and former colleagues but she has always stayed in contact with family. Moreover, our families support us whatever we do. They help us when times are difficult and take genuine pleasure in our successes.

This does not mean that I believe that relationships with friends and colleagues are unimportant. Quite the opposite is true. In some ways, we may have more in common with people who work or study in the same place. In contrast, our colleagues and fellow students tend to have similar goals in life to our own.

In conclusion, I think that all relationships are important to people’s happiness. We need friends and colleagues as well as family. But, as the saying goes, blood is thicker than water, and family relationships in the end matter more when it comes to living a happy life.

 Reading Practice • Questions 1–6 Clothes and Identity: Matching Information (A–F)

Clothes and Identity: We Are What We Wear

[Paragraph A] We all communicate through fashion. Our clothes, accessories, hairstyles and make-up are all things we use to promote ourselves to others. Fashion is about meanings and symbols that give us immediate visual communication. But I would suggest it is also about decision-making, performance and fitting into society.

[Paragraph B] It is certainly true that fashion is linked to how we view each other. We make decisions based on a person’s appearance within a second. Clothing style gives information about a person’s character, sociability and intelligence. Attractive people generate a "halo effect", which makes us believe everything connected with that person is positive. Consequently, we often think attractive people are more intelligent, healthy and sociable compared with unattractive people. In a recent study supported by a tailoring company, participants were shown images of a man for three seconds. The first image showed him in a suit made especially for him. The second showed him in a suit bought from a high-street shop. People thought the man in the first image was more confident, successful and a higher earner than in the second.

[Paragraph C] Researchers have also looked at the difference between wanting what we have and having what we want, and they made an interesting discovery. They found that we get more pleasure from buying objects than we do from owning the objects themselves. We enjoy shopping because it allows us to compare ourselves with others. Our new Prada shoes may be lovely, but our pleasure in them depends on impressing others. You’d think that buyer’s guilt would stop us shopping but it doesn’t. In fact, psychologists found that feeling guilty often pushes us to shop more. And when we can’t find what we want, we are motivated to try even harder because our brains release the chemical dopamine which gives us pleasure. The longer it takes us to find what we want, the more dopamine our brain releases.

[Paragraph D] The shopping experience is not always the same for both genders, however. From a young age, women are judged on their appearance, and men on skills and abilities. Fashion can be used to change appearance, self-identity and boost confidence by showing some qualities and hiding others. It is therefore not surprising that shopping is more popular among women. It is beginning to change but in a 2013 paper, researchers found 80 percent of men hate shopping with their partner, and 45 percent try not to go at all costs. Half of couples fight when they shop together, as men claim they get bored, hungry or thirsty and wish they were outside. If we compare this to the past when men hunted alone, as fast as possible, and women spent time with each other selecting berries, nuts and vegetables, we can see shopping is the same. Women take their time and do it with friends. Men prefer to shop alone and fast.

[Paragraph E] Clothes and accessories are not only connected with our identity; they also affect how we feel and perform. When we give meaning to the clothes we wear, we can improve our cognitive abilities, which can influence our social interactions. Researchers found that simply wearing a lab coat increased performance on attention-related tasks. And, more interestingly, when it was described as a doctor’s coat as opposed to a painter’s, sustained attention increased even more. Psychologists call this effect "enclothed cognition".

[Paragraph F] Finally, fashion can allow us to fit into a group or stand out as different. Groups have a "uniform" that identifies membership (e.g., punk, City worker). Our desire to be part of the group will influence the way we dress, and following the latest trends can give us membership of the "fashionable" group. This is a problem for fashion, as people think it is about looking different. But once an item becomes fashionable, it is no longer different. Uniforms are frequently adopted by fashion and other industry leaders. For example, fashion magazine editor Anna Wintour only wears printed sleeveless dresses, and famous model Kate Moss is addicted to skinny jeans and ankle boots. Wearing the same clothes every day means you do not have to worry about what to wear and people immediately recognise you.

⚡ Intensifier Collocation Rules

• Regular Adjectives: modified by very, really, extremely, incredibly (e.g. very anxious, extremely proud).
• Extreme Adjectives: modified by absolutely (e.g. absolutely furious, absolutely terrified, absolutely exhausted).
• Versatile Modifier: really works with both (really tired / really exhausted).

Task Prompt

"Both men and women care more about their appearance today than they did in the past. To what extent do you agree or disagree with this statement?"

Detailed Essay Plan
• Intro: Paraphrase prompt + clear thesis (women remained constant, men increased).
• Body 1 (Skin care): Past female dominance vs modern male grooming products.
• Body 2 (Media role): Role of male celebrities in music, sports, and fashion.
• Conclusion: Reiterate equality of appearance focus today.
Band 6+ Official Model Answer

For centuries, people have taken care of the way they dress. However, traditionally it has been women who are more concerned with their appearance than men. This essay will argue that women’s attitudes to appearance have not changed in recent decades, whereas men have become more concerned with how they look.

One reason for this is the growing importance of skin care. In the past, only women cared about their skin. They were the ones who put on cream each day to keep their faces looking young and wrinkle-free. Men used shaving products but nothing more. Today, they feel the pressure to have soft and young-looking skin too, and so there are now skin-care products especially for men, which are very popular among the younger generation.

Another reason for men caring more about their appearance is the role of the male celebrity. In the past, female celebrities were often famous because of their looks, but male celebrities were people who were clever or skilled. Their appearance was less important than their actions, but today it is often the opposite. Men see male celebrities and feel that they should try to look as good.

In conclusion, society has always felt that women should look attractive and, while body image is an issue today, this has not necessarily changed. Men, however, are required to pay much more attention to their appearance these days so they have good skin and look as good as celebrity role models. Appearance is now equally important for both men and women.
