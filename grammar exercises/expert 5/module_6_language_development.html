Expert Grammar Page 188 Reference Guide

Future Forms: be going to

Comprehensive reference on forming intentions, talking about existing decisions, and predicting outcomes from visible present evidence.

FORM

Grammar Form

am / is / are + going to + bare infinitive

1. Affirmative (+)

Subject + am / is / are + going to + verb

"I'm going to cook dinner tonight."

2. Negative (-)

Subject + 'm not / isn't / aren't + going to + verb

"They aren't going to shop there any longer."

3. Questions (?)

(Wh-) + am / is / are + subject + going to + verb?

"Are you going to eat out this weekend?"

USE

Usage & Contrast with will

Pre-planned intentions vs Instant decisions

1. Planned Decisions:

Use be going to when you decided before the time of speaking.

"I'm going to buy my vegetables at the market."

(Compare: "The phone is ringing – I'll answer it." → instant decision uses will)

2. Predictions with Present Evidence:

Use be going to when physical signs in the present indicate what will occur.

"Look at the prices! This meal is going to be expensive."

3. Verbs of Movement (go / come):

With go or come, native speakers often use the present continuous instead of repeating going to go:

"I'm going to the market." (or "I'm going to go to the market.")
