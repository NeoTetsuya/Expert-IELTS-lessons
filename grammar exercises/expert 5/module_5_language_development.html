Expert Grammar Page 187 Reference Guide

-ing Forms and Infinitives

Comprehensive guide to using gerunds, full infinitives with to, bare infinitives, and verbs with shifting or dual patterns.

-ING

-ing Forms (Gerunds)

Nouns, Prepositions & Verb Complements

1. As the subject or object of a sentence:

"Travelling by train is more comfortable than flying."

"Walking around a town is the best way to explore."

2. After prepositions:

"I always feel sad after returning from holiday."

"I'm good at learning new languages."

"Instead of lying on a beach..."

3. After certain verbs:

admit, avoid, can't help, can't stand, consider, deny, dislike, enjoy, fancy, finish, imagine, insist on, look forward to, mind, miss, practise, risk, suggest.

+TO

Infinitives with to

Purpose, Adjectives & Specific Verbs

1. To express purpose:

"Last year I went to Egypt to see the pyramids."

"I usually call a travel agent to book my holiday."

2. After adjectives:

"I find it difficult to sleep on a plane."

"It can be difficult to decide where to go."

3. After certain verbs:

afford, agree, appear, arrange, choose, decide, expect, fail, help, hope, learn, manage, offer, plan, promise, refuse, seem, want, would like / prefer.

⚡ Bare Infinitives (without to) & Verbs with Dual Patterns

Infinitive without to (Bare Infinitive)
  • After modal auxiliary verbs: can, could, might, must, should, will, would (e.g. "We might visit the museums.").
  • After make and let + object: "My parents made me go camping." / "They let us walk round."
  • After would rather: "I would rather travel by train."
Verbs with Little or No Meaning Change

Verbs like begin, continue, start, like, love, prefer, hate can take either an -ing form or a to-infinitive with virtually no difference in meaning:

✓ "They generally prefer staying / to stay in hotels."

✓ "I started learning / to learn Spanish."
