Public Speaking: Subject vs. Object Relative Pronouns

When a relative pronoun is the object of the verb in a defining relative clause, it can be omitted. Click on the 4 relative pronouns below that can be omitted!

✓ CAN be omitted (Object Pronouns):

When the relative pronoun is followed by a subject noun or pronoun: "something (which) politicians do", "the product (which) you sell".

✗ CANNOT be omitted (Subject / Whose):

When the relative pronoun is the subject followed immediately by a verb: "a skill which is difficult", "people who find...", or whose.

Relative Clauses: Defining & Non-defining

The definitive guide to relative pronouns (who, which, that, where, whose), pronoun omission rules, and avoiding pronoun repetition.

RC

Relative Pronouns in Defining Clauses

Essential identifying information

1. People (who / that):

"Having a conversation with someone who / that is older than you..."

2. Things (which / that):

"Email is the main form of communication which / that is used in business."

3. Places (where) & Possession (whose):

"I prefer to work in places where I can get internet access."

"Students whose phones ring during class will be asked to leave."

RULE

Omission & Avoiding Repetition

Key Exam Focus Points

1. Omitting the Relative Pronoun:

When the relative pronoun is the object of the clause, it can be left out:

"The emails (which) I received while I was away."

(If followed immediately by a verb, it is the subject and CANNOT be omitted!)

2. Avoiding Redundant Pronouns:

Never repeat the subject or object pronoun in the relative clause:

✗ "He has an accent I find difficult to understand it."

✓ "He has an accent I find difficult to understand."

⚡ Summary of Relative Pronoun Usage

Pronoun Refers to Can be replaced by 'that'? Can be omitted?
who people Yes (in defining clauses) Yes (when it is the object)
which things / animals Yes (in defining clauses) Yes (when it is the object)
where places No No
whose possession (people or things) No No
