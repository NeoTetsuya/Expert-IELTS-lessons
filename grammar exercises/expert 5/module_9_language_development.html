Expert Grammar Page 190 Reference Guide

Present Perfect vs. Past Simple

The essential guide to distinguishing finished time (Past Simple) from connection with the present moment (Present Perfect).

PP

Present Perfect Simple

Connected to Present / Unfinished

1. Unfinished Time Periods:

Used with time periods that include right now: this week, today, this month, this year.

"I've read three newspapers this week."

2. Life Experience & Indefinite Past:

The exact time is unknown, unimportant, or not stated: ever, never, already, yet.

"I've seen that film – it's good."

3. Continuing States & Situations:

Started in past, continuing now: with since (starting point) or for (duration).

"I've been on Facebook since 2010."

PS

Past Simple

Finished Actions at Specific Times

1. Finished Time Periods:

Used with completed past time markers: yesterday, last year, two days ago, in 2011, when.

"I read the news online yesterday."

2. Specific Past Events in Sequence:

Narrating completed actions in news reports or stories:

"In 2011, reporters did not break the news. Social media users told the world."

3. Asking "When / What time?":

Questions asking for a specific point in time always use the Past Simple:

"When did you start using this app?" (NOT: When have you started?)

⚡ Summary of Time Words & Markers

Tense Characteristic Time Markers Example Sentences
Present Perfect already, yet, just, ever, never, since 2010, for years, lately, recently, this week, so far "I haven't seen the evening news yet."
"He has read the paper since he was 18."

"I saw the broadcast at lunchtime."
