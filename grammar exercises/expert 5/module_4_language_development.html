Past Simple: Completed Actions & Specific Time

We use the past simple for finished actions, events, or situations that occurred at a specific, known point in the past. Look for past time markers like yesterday, last year, and ago.

1 Read the sentences. Are the actions complete? How do you know?

Sentence 1

"I lost weight last year when I started exercising."

Action status: Finished. Specific time: last year.

Sentence 2

"Al didn't go to work yesterday because he was ill."

Action status: Finished. Specific time: yesterday.

💡 Analysis:

Yes, both actions are completely finished. We know this because the verbs are in the Past Simple (lost, started, didn't go, was) and specific finished time expressions (last year, yesterday) explicitly anchor them in the past.

Expert Grammar Page 185 Reference Guide

Past Simple & Past Continuous Reference

Comprehensive grammatical guidelines for expressing finished past events, ongoing states, background actions, and interrupted timelines.

PS

Past Simple

Finished Actions at a Specific Time

1. Finished actions & events:

"I lost weight last year when I started exercising."

"Al didn't go to work yesterday because he was ill."

2. Past habits and repeated routines:

"When I was younger, I went swimming regularly."

"In the past, people had more active jobs."

3. Time expressions commonly used:

yesterday, last week/month/year, two years ago, in 1995, in the past, when I was...

PC

Past Continuous

Actions in Progress at a Specific Time

1. Action in progress at a precise point in time:

"At 8 o'clock this morning I was working out at the gym."

"At 10 p.m. last night I was watching TV."

2. Two actions happening at the same time:

"They were using up a lot of energy while they were running."

3. Setting the scene / background in a story:

"The sun was just rising and it was starting to get warm."

⚡ Interrupted Actions: Using when and while

Long Action + Short Interruption

We use the Past Continuous for the longer background action in progress, and the Past Simple for the shorter action that interrupted it:

"While I was walking in the park, it suddenly started to rain."
State Verbs in the Past

Stative verbs (e.g. be, have (=possess), know, understand, like) are virtually never used in the continuous form, even when describing background situations:

✓ "When I was younger, I had a healthier diet."
✗ NOT: When I was being younger...
