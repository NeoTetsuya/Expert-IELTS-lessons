Started in the past and is still ongoing right now (often with for or since).

Exercise 4 Duration (for) vs. Starting Point (since)

Distinguishing "for" and "since"

Use for with a period / duration of time (e.g., for three years, for a long time). Use since with a specific point in time or starting event (e.g., since March, since we won).

⏱️ FOR + Period of Time

for over five years, for the last three years, for a long time, for two weeks, for an hour

📍 SINCE + Fixed Point / Event

since March, since the beginning of term, since we won the championships, since Tuesday

Expert Grammar Page 189 Reference Guide

Present Perfect Simple: Form, Meaning & Adverbs

Comprehensive reference on connecting the past to the present: accomplishments, indefinite past actions, continuing states, and contrast with the Past Simple.

FORM

Grammar Form

have / has + past participle

1. Affirmative (+)

Subject + have / has + past participle

"She has played in many international matches."

2. Negative (-)

Subject + haven't / hasn't + past participle

"We haven't had time to practise recently."

3. Questions (?)

(Question word) + Have / Has + subject + past participle?

"Have you ever seen a live rugby match?"

USE

Key Uses & Time Expressions

Connecting Past to Present

1. Continuing States (for & since):

for + duration (for five years) | since + starting point (since 2015, since March).

2. Life Experiences (ever & never):

"Have you ever played badminton?" | "I've never swum in a lake."

3. Recent Events (just, already, yet, recently, lately):

"I have just started." | "I have already been." | "I haven't done any exercise yet."

4. Present Perfect vs. Past Simple:

Use Past Simple when the time is finished or specified (yesterday, in 2010). Use Present Perfect when the time period is unfinished (this week, today, in my life).
