1b What do the gapped words in sentences (1–6) refer to?

1. 'can' refers to:

"...understand art" (modal auxiliary ellipsis avoids repeating the whole predicate).

2. 'that / this' refers to:

"colour" (referring back to the specific element mentioned previously).

3. 'this method' & 'this' refer to:

The technique of using "vertical lines", and "the painting / scene itself".

4. 'these' & 'do so' refer to:

"these" = the lines of the train tracks; "do so" = follow the lines with our eyes.

5. 'These / Such features' refers to:

"the top lines of the roofs and the direction of the track" grouped together.

6. 'so' refers to:

"relax the image" (substituting for the clause/adjective phrase to express degree).

💡 Cohesion Mechanism Analysis:

These devices ensure text flows naturally without annoying verbatim repetition. Ellipsis leaves words out when the meaning is obvious (1). Demonstratives (this, that, these, those) point backward or forward (2, 3, 4, 5). Substitution uses general words like so or do so to replace entire verb phrases (4, 6).

Eliminating Repetition: Restructuring for Academic Flow

4a IELTS Essay Prompt Analysis

"Should we invest in looking after old buildings, or should we replace them when they are no longer functional?"

Arguments FOR Preserving Old Buildings:
  • They embody historical identity and cultural heritage.
  • Architectural uniqueness attracts tourism revenue.
  • Embodied carbon in existing structures is conserved.
Arguments FOR Replacing Them:
  • Exorbitant continuous repair and maintenance costs.
  • Poor insulation leads to severe carbon waste and high energy bills.
  • Modern buildings accommodate disabled access and modern tech better.
✗

Original Unedited Extract (Repetitive)

Score: Band 5.5

I think that we should look after the old buildings in our city. The old buildings represent our history and our cultural past. Representing our cultural past is important because it makes our cities look unique. When we make our cities look unique, they are more attractive to tourists, and being attractive to tourists is good for tourism.

However, it can be expensive to run a very old building. Very old buildings often need a lot of repairs. Very old buildings often don't have the latest windows, doors and insulation. Not having the latest windows doors and insulation means that they are inefficient. Being inefficient is bad for the environment as well as our wallets.

⚠️ Notice the heavy repetition of identical noun phrases and clumsy gerund clauses chained back-to-back.

✓

Cohesive Academic Model (Band 8.5+)

High Cohesion

I think that we should look after the old buildings in our city, as these represent our history and cultural heritage. This connection is important because it lends our cities a unique character, which in turn makes them more appealing to visitors and boosts the local economy.

However, maintaining such historic structures can be extremely expensive. Not only do they frequently require costly repairs, but they also lack modern insulation, windows and doors. This inefficiency harms both the environment and public finances.

✨ Uses: as these (demonstrative pronoun), This connection (summary noun), which in turn (relative clause), such historic structures (determiner + synonym), and This inefficiency (nominalisation).

Expert Grammar Page 176 Reference Guide

Grammar to Connect Ideas

Mastering reference pronouns, substitution, ellipsis, demonstratives, and summary noun phrases for high-level coherence and cohesion in academic English.

RD

Reference & Demonstratives

Pointing Backward & Forward

1. This, That, These, Those:

This / These: Refer to things just mentioned or situations physically/temporally close: "These shapes help to relax the image."

That / Those: Refer to things further away in time, or classes of people: "For those with the money..." / "At that time..."

2. Such:

Means "of this type / so great": "Few people have the money to meet such high yearly costs."

"Such numbers might be enough to persuade you..."

SE

Substitution & Ellipsis

Avoiding Direct Repetition

1. Substitution with 'so' and 'do so':

do so: Replaces a verb and its object: "He asked us to follow the lines. If we do so, we can see..."

so: Replaces a clause or adjective of degree: "...perhaps less so than the colour."

2. Modal Ellipsis:

Leaving out the main verb after an auxiliary: "Many people believe they can't understand art, but they can [understand art]."

⚡ Summary Nouns: Encapsulating Ideas

In IELTS Academic Writing, replacing a whole previous clause with a summary noun (this problem, such initiatives, this change, these improvements) is the hallmark of Band 8+ cohesion:

Actions ➔ Nouns

Opening later & interactive screens ➔ "Such recent improvements have resulted in..."

Descriptions ➔ Nouns

Beautiful arches & sculptures ➔ "This stunning architecture has made it..."

Mindsets ➔ Nouns

Seeing their role as entertainment ➔ "This change in attitude has made..."
