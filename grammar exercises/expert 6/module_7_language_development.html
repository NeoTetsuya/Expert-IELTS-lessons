1b Condition Connectors & Tense Analysis:

Condition Introduction Words:

• If: Introduces a possible condition (1 & 3).
• Otherwise: Means "if we don't" or "or else" (2).
• Provided that: Means "only if / on condition that" (4).
• Unless: Means "if not / except if" (5).

Tense Structure Patterns:

• In the condition clause: We use the present simple (or present continuous), NOT will: look, have, take, has.
• In the result clause: We use will / won't + base verb, or a modal verb (can), or an imperative.

💡 Notice the punctuation:

When the condition clause comes first (Sentences 1, 3, 4), a comma separates it from the result clause. When the result clause comes first (Sentences 2, 5), no comma is required before unless or otherwise (unless preceded by a semicolon or period).

Expert Grammar Page 178 Reference Guide

Zero and First Conditional

Comprehensive reference on conditional structures, clause order, alternative connectors (unless, provided that, otherwise, even if), and modal variations.

0C

Zero Conditional

Universal Truths, Habits & Facts

1. Form: If / When + present simple, present simple

"If you heat water to 100°C, it boils."

"When people get stressed, their heart rate increases."

2. Real, permanent situations:

Used for outcomes that always occur whenever the condition is met. If and when have virtually the same meaning here.

1C

First Conditional

Real Possibilities & Future Consequences

1. Form: If + present simple, will / won't + infinitive

"If air travel gets cheaper, more people will fly."

✗ NEVER use will in the if-clause: If it will get cheaper...

2. Variations in the result clause:

• Modal verbs: can, could, may, might, should (e.g. "If you take a boat, you can cross more quickly.")

• Imperatives: "If you need assistance, press the button."

⚡ Alternative Condition Conjunctions Reference

Unless (= if not)

"There will be traffic problems unless we invest in roads." (if we don't invest).

Provided that (= only if)

"Provided that space agencies share ideas, technology will advance." (on condition that).

Otherwise (= or else)

"We need radical plans, otherwise cities will come to a standstill."

Even if (= whether or not)

"Even if traffic gets worse, cars will still be popular."
