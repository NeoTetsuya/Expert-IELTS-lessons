Exercise 3a, 3b & 3c Noun & Adjective Patterns for Probability

Alternative Probability Structures

Elevate your academic English by moving between modal verbs and formal phrases like "There is a strong possibility that..." and "It is highly likely that...".

3a Underline the language of probability in each pair. Is the meaning the same? How is the structure different?

Pair 1

A: Teleportation will probably not work with humans.

B: There is a high probability that teleportation will not work with humans.

Meaning: Identical high-probability prediction.

Pair 2

A: Humans will probably be able to travel across the universe.

B: There is a strong possibility that humans will be able to travel across the universe.

Meaning: Identical likelihood of interplanetary travel.

Pair 3

A: The teleportation of objects will almost definitely become a reality.

B: It is highly likely that the teleportation of objects will become a reality.

Meaning: Identical near-certain expectation.

💡 Structural Distinction:

Sentence A uses modal auxiliary verbs and adverbs directly modifying the verb (will + adverb + base verb).
Sentence B uses impersonal noun structures (There is a [high/strong] probability/possibility that + clause) or anticipatory 'It' adjective structures (It is [highly/quite] likely that + clause). Both forms convey the exact same degree of certainty!

Expert Grammar Page 176 Reference Guide

Future & Present Probability Reference

Mastering modal verbs, adverbs of certainty, word order rules, and noun/adjective probability structures for IELTS and Cambridge exams.

FP

Future Probability

Modals & Adverbs of Certainty

1. Degrees of certainty:

100% sure: will definitely (positive) / definitely won't or will definitely not (negative).

70–80% sure: will probably / probably won't or will probably not.

30–50% not sure: may, might, could, will possibly / may not, might not, possibly won't.

2. Critical word order rule:

In the positive, adverbs go after will: "It will definitely happen."

With won't, adverbs go before won't: "It definitely won't happen."

With will not, adverbs go between: "It will definitely not happen."

PP

Present Probability

Modal Deduction (Now)

1. Certain it is true: must

"Physics must be one of the most challenging subjects. It's very complex."

2. Certain it is impossible: can't

"Scientists can't possibly know the future; they're just guessing."

✗ NEVER use mustn't for logical deduction of impossibility.

3. Possible now: could / may / might

"Overuse of technology might be the cause of our brains getting smaller."

⚡ Academic Formulas: Noun Phrases & Anticipatory 'It'

Probability

"There is a high / low probability that teleportation will (not) work with humans."

Possibility / Chance

"There is a strong / small possibility (or chance) that humans will travel across the universe."

Likelihood

"It is highly / quite / unlikely that the teleportation of objects will become a reality."
