Exercise 2 & 3 Periods of Time vs. Starting Points

Duration: For vs. Since & World Climate Records

Use for with periods/duration of time (e.g. for three decades) and since with starting points (e.g. since 1913, since November).

Rule 1: FOR Period of Time

"People have lived there for around 800 years."

Use for to express the length or duration of time up to now.

Rule 2: SINCE Starting Point

"People have lived there since around 1200."

Use since with a specific point in the past when the action began.

Exercise 4a, 4b & 4c IELTS Academic Writing Task 1 Tense Contrast

Present Perfect vs. Past Simple in Climate Data

In Academic Task 1, use the present perfect when a trend connects past data to the present (e.g. has risen since 1972), and the past simple for completed historical spans (e.g. between 2001 and 2016).

4a Why does the speaker use the past simple in the second clause?

"Although people have lived in Yakutsk for centuries, the population grew quickly after 1880 because of gold mining."

💡 Analysis:

The first clause uses the present perfect (have lived) because the habitation started in the past and continues right up to today. The second clause uses the past simple (grew) because "after 1880" specifies a finished historical time period when the rapid gold-rush boom took place.

Expert Grammar Page 177 Reference Guide

Present Perfect Simple Reference

Form, usage categories, time expressions (for vs. since), adverb placement, and contrast with the past simple in IELTS examinations.

PPS

Core Uses & Form

has / have + past participle

1. Past action with present result / unspecified time:

"There are towns that have recorded over -60°C." (The focus is on the record and its present reality, not the precise date).

2. Ongoing states & actions:

"The Yakutsk people have lived in that region for around 800 years." (Started 800 years ago and they still live there now).

3. Recent actions:

"A gale has just started blowing." (Happened moments ago).

FS

For vs. Since

Duration & Starting Points

1. FOR + Period of Time:

for three decades, for a long time, for hours, for several months, for a few seconds.

Answers the question: How long?

2. SINCE + Fixed Starting Point:

since 1913, since around 1200, since November, since last year, since the beginning of the month.

Answers the question: Since when?

⚡ Crucial Contrast: Present Perfect vs. Past Simple

Present Perfect (Open / Current)

Used when the time period is unfinished or connected to the present:
"Average summer temperatures have risen significantly since 1972." (The trend continues today).

Past Simple (Finished Historical Span)

Used when a specific, finished time period is mentioned:
"There was a noticeable increase between 2001 and 2016." / "Last year alone, it increased by 0.15°C."
