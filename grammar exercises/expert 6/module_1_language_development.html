Click any question to listen and view the tense explanation:

Question 1

"Are you working or studying at the moment?"

Grammar Tense: Present Continuous

Why? "At the moment" refers to current ongoing life activity, not a permanent habit.

Question 2

"What do you like best about your course?"

Grammar Tense: Present Simple

Why? "Like" is a stative verb expressing a permanent preference or opinion.

Question 3

"Is there anything you DISLIKE about your course?"

Grammar Tense: Present Simple

Why? "Dislike" describes an emotional attitude; it is virtually never used in continuous aspect.

Question 4

"What are you planning to do when you finish your course?"

Grammar Tense: Present Continuous

Why? Asking about plans that are currently taking shape for future execution.

Expert Grammar Page 173 Reference Guide

Present Tenses: Simple vs Continuous

Master the formal rules governing state verbs, habitual routines, temporary situations, and ongoing trends for the IELTS test.

PS

Present Simple

Regular Habits & Permanent Facts

1. Regular habits, routines & schedules:

"Do you use your phone for games regularly?"

"The majority never visits the travel agent's."

2. Permanent situations & general truths:

"The study of online moods is a large field."

"Bad moods online appear more frequently."

3. Stative verbs (feelings, thoughts, possession):

"What does that include?" (NOT is that including)

"All you need to do is sign here."

PC

Present Continuous

Happening Now, Temporary & Trends

1. Actions happening now / around now:

"I am looking for a new phone plan."

"I'm studying for my IELTS exam at the moment."

2. Temporary situations for a limited period:

"We are offering it at that price for a short period."

3. Trends & evolving social changes:

"Times are changing fast."

"More people are turning to technology."

⚡ Common Stative Verbs Reference (Rarely used in Continuous form)

Thoughts & Opinions

think, believe, know, understand, remember, agree, realize, suppose

Feelings & Preferences

like, love, hate, dislike, want, need, prefer, wish, hope

Perception & Senses

appear, seem, sound, taste, smell, look (=seem), hear, recognize

Possession & Measure

include, belong, contain, own, have (=possess), cost, weigh, exist
