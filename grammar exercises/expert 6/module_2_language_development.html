Exercise 4a, 4b & 4c Academic Writing Task 2

Writing Academic Paragraphs with Relative Clauses

Short, choppy sentences sound informal. Combining ideas with relative clauses elevates your IELTS Grammatical Range and Accuracy score (Band 7.0+).

✓ Positive Effects of Stress (Eustress):

  • Enhances cognitive focus and acute alertness under pressure.
  • Releases neurochemicals that temporarily heighten memory recall.
  • Provides energy spikes needed to meet critical professional deadlines.

✗ Negative Effects of Chronic Stress (Distress):

  • Elevated cortisol resulting in persistent fatigue and sleep disruption.
  • Reduced immune resistance, migraines, and high blood pressure.
  • Mental burnout and irritability when pressures become sustained.

Cohesion & Flow

"Stress is a common emotion. It can have both a positive and negative effect on a person. It can help you achieve success. For example, it gives you motivation and energy. You need them to complete tasks well. In addition, stress can help your memory. When you are stressed, the brain releases chemicals. They help you to focus better. A person is probably happier in general. He or she is focused and successful."

Academic Rewritten Version: Band 7.5+ Style

"Stress is a common emotion which can have both a positive and negative effect on a person by helping them achieve success. For example, it provides motivation and energy that are needed to complete tasks well. In addition, stress can improve memory because, under pressure, the brain produces neurochemicals which help individuals focus better. Consequently, a person who is focused and successful is generally happier."

Expert Grammar Page 174 Reference Guide

Defining Relative Clauses

Defining relative clauses provide essential information that identifies exactly which person, thing, place, or time we are referring to. No commas are used!

who / that For people

"People who hear about the research make an effort."

which / that For things & objects

"It is the machine that lets you take money out."

where For places

"Visit websites where you can see photos."

when For times & moments

"Things seem easier when you're happier."

SO

Subject vs. Object Pronouns

Syntactic Role

1. As the SUBJECT of the relative clause:

The relative pronoun is followed directly by a verb. It cannot be omitted.

✓ The plum trees which are starting to bloom.

✗ NOT: The plum trees are starting to bloom. (incomplete sentence without conjunction)

2. As the OBJECT of the relative clause:

The relative pronoun is followed by a subject + verb. It can be omitted in informal English.

✓ The smile of someone (who) you are close to.

PR

Prepositions & Formal Academic Style

Positioning Rules

1. Informal / Spoken English (Preposition at the end):

Prepositions usually sit at the end of the clause after the verb.

✓ "...someone that you're close to."

✓ "...the people I work with."

2. Formal Academic English (Preposition before pronoun):

In formal Task 2 writing, prepositions precede whom (for people) or which (for things). Note: that cannot follow a preposition directly.

✓ "...someone to whom you are close."

✓ "...the environment in which they operate."
