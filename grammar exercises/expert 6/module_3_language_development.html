Expert Grammar Page 175 Reference Guide

Comparative and Superlative Forms

Master the rules governing comparative adjectives, adverbs, modifiers of degree, and proportional quantifiers for IELTS Academic.

One-Syllable -er / -est

dull ➔ duller ➔ dullest
old ➔ older ➔ oldest

Ending in -y -ier / -iest

easy ➔ easier ➔ easiest
early ➔ earlier ➔ earliest

Multi-Syllable more / most

popular ➔ more popular
accurately ➔ more accurately

Irregulars good / bad / far

good ➔ better ➔ best
bad ➔ worse ➔ worst

MD

Modifying Comparatives

Expressing Degree

1. Large differences:

Use much, far, a lot, significantly, considerably.

✓ "...identify routine illness much more accurately."

✓ "History was far less engaging."

2. Small differences:

Use slightly, a little, a bit, marginally.

✓ "...numbers were slightly higher than the year before."

PR

Quantities & Proportions

Multipliers & Negation

1. Equal & Negative Comparisons:

as ... as (equal) vs not as/so ... as (less than).

✓ "History is now just as popular as geography."

✓ "There weren't as many subjects in the past."

2. Numerical Proportions:

Use twice as many/much as, three times as many as, half as many/much as.

✓ "They had twice as many students taking those exams."

✓ "In the past, around half as many students chose history."
