Comprehensive Buying & Selling Lexicon

Full reference definitions, phonetic transcriptions, grammatical collocations, and one-click British English pronunciation.

chemist's

noun [C]

A shop where medicinal drugs, toiletries, soap, and cosmetic products are prepared and sold.

Collocation: pick up a prescription at the chemist's

newsagent's

noun [C]

A shop that primarily sells newspapers, magazines, sweets, tobacco, and confectionary.

Collocation: buy the morning paper at the newsagent's

antique shop

noun [C]

A specialty store that sells valuable old objects, paintings, vintage jewellery, and furniture.

Collocation: browse rare clocks in an antique shop

sales assistant

compound noun [C]

A staff member in a retail store whose job is to serve customers, answer questions, and process payments.

Collocation: ask a helpful sales assistant

fitting room

compound noun [C]

A private enclosure in a clothes shop where customers can try on clothes before purchasing them.

Collocation: queue for the fitting room

receipt vs. bill

commerce distinction

A bill states the money you *must pay* (e.g. at a restaurant). A receipt is proof that you have *already paid*.

Example: check the restaurant bill / keep the shop receipt

afford vs. cost

verb usage

A buyer affords something (has enough money). An item costs a particular sum of money.

Example: can't afford the jacket / it costs £120

browse vs. spend

verb usage

To browse means looking through items casually without deciding to buy. To spend is using money.

Example: browse in a bookstore / spend £30 on books

in stock & on offer

retail idioms

In stock: goods present in the warehouse/store. On special offer: items temporarily sold at a bargain price.

Example: product is in stock on special offer
